Who it's for. For any household that wants to be able to alert others and be alerted when every network goes down at once: a long blackout, a DANA storm, a fire or a lockdown.

When it isn't enough. It doesn't replace your phone day to day, and it won't let you make long-distance calls without an amateur radio licence. And it's not a toy: if you don't test it beforehand, you won't know how to use it on the day of the emergency.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Not testing the walkie-talkies beforehand: an unused device is an expensive brick.
  2. Buying walkie-talkies that aren't PMR446: many cheap devices use bands that do require a license.
  3. Relying entirely on the satellite messenger: if it runs out of battery, you're left unable to communicate.
  4. Storing the batteries inside the radio: they corrode and ruin the device.
  5. Calling instead of texting: in an emergency, voice calls fail before short messages.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Agree a fixed family channel and sub-tone (channel 7, sub-tone 7) and write it on each walkie-talkie
  • Test the real range: one at home and one out on the street; in the city it reaches far less than the box promises
  • Tune in and save the useful stations (regional and Radio Nacional) before putting the radio away
  • Keep spare batteries out of the devices and a short cable with the powerbank
  • If you have a satellite messenger, set up the emergency contacts and test sending a message

→ During

  • Listen before transmitting: two stations at once block the channel with a screech
  • Short messages and speak slowly; repeat figures and addresses twice
  • Radio on at least twice a day to hear official warnings
  • Don't clog the mobile network with calls: texts get through when calls don't
  • Agree a fixed contact time so you don't drain the battery all day

→ After

  • Recharge the walkie-talkies, powerbank and messenger, and replace used batteries
  • Note down what failed (range, channel with interference) and fix it
  • Check your satellite subscription if you have one: don't let it lapse
