Who it's for. Households with people aged 70 or over, especially if they live alone, take chronic medication or rely on hearing aids, glasses or assisted mobility. A family member assembles it in an afternoon and reviews it with the prescription folder.

When it isn't enough. It does not replace telecare or the caregiver plan: it is materials, not a service. And if the person requires daily healthcare (oxygen, dialysis), the plan is discussed with their health center, not with this list.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Not having 2 weeks' supply of chronic medication in reserve. It is THE mistake: after a disaster, the pharmacy opens late or does not open, and stopping anticoagulants, antihypertensives or insulin is an emergency.
  2. Forgetting hearing aid batteries: without hearing, the person does not hear the radio, does not understand the warning and does not ask for help. A blister pack of spare batteries in every kit.
  3. Relying on the mobile phone: small screens, small print and no one to help them dial. The landline still works without electricity (analog ones do) and paper contacts never run out.
  4. Not preparing for the fall: without light, a carpeted hallway with no night light means a broken hip. LED night lights cost €10 and prevent 50% of that scenario.
  5. Storing everything in the high kitchen cupboard. If you evacuate, the older adult's 24-hour bag must be within easy reach, with name and contacts.
  6. Not planning for incontinence. Without a washing machine or hot water, a single day without enough spare pads forces a choice between hygiene and drinking water. €15-30 in the cupboard solves it long before it's needed.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Make the medical ID card together with the person: conditions, medication with doses, allergies, doctor, contacts. Renew it every 6 months during the review.
  • Ask the doctor or nurse for a 2-week plan of chronic medication: in Spain, the electronic prescription allows most treatments to be collected in advance; your doctor will tell you how.
  • Practice using the card and the telecare phone with the person, not just in front of them: in an emergency, 'they already know' is not enough.
  • Agree with neighbors and family who calls whom and what warning signal you use (call, message, knocking on the wall: during power cuts some people use a whistle).
  • Count how many times they get up at night to use the bathroom: if it is more than once, a commode with a handle, a night light in the hallway and a non-slip mat come before any other product on the list.
  • Store medication in a bag with a handle that the person can hang over their shoulder, not in stacked boxes: in a quick exit, you only grab what you can pick up with one hand.

→ During

  • Medication first: if you have to decide, chronic treatment is not skipped; the kit's reserve is for exactly that.
  • With hearing aids out of batteries or broken glasses, the person becomes isolated and disoriented: check that they can hear and see before anything else.
  • Heat or cold: older people regulate temperature worse and do not notice thirst. In a heatwave without power, keep water in sight and set a drinking schedule, even if they don't ask.
  • Do not let them make decisions alone if they are confused: heatstroke and dehydration cause confusion before weakness.
  • Without a washing machine or hot water, incontinence is the day-2 problem: use absorbent pads and a separate bag for soiled clothes. Do not hand-wash with the reserve water: that water is for drinking.
  • If the phone fails and she lives alone, a whistle and rhythmic knocks on the wall are how a neighbor can locate her. Agree it in advance with the neighbor and with her, using a specific signal (three knocks, pause, three knocks).

→ After

  • Check medication that has been used and restock the kit the same day with the prescription.
  • Check hearing aid batteries, pacemaker batteries if applicable, and flashlights: in the scare, many things get used without being replaced.
  • Talk to the health center if treatment was interrupted: sometimes it has to be prescribed again.
  • Encourage them to talk about what they experienced: after a long emergency, an older person's emotional crash is as real as a burst pipe.
  • Check the skin in folds and pressure areas if there has been more than 24 hours without being able to wash: an irritation or a red area that does not recover should be discussed with their nurse or doctor, not treated at home.
  • Note down which product was actually missing and restock it that same day: the kit is corrected with what was used, not with what you thought you would use.
