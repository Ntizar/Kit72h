Who it's for. For anyone living in a seismic zone (south-east mainland Spain: Murcia, Almería, Granada, Málaga, Alicante) and, in fact, for any home: most earthquake damage doesn't come from the quake itself, it comes from falling objects and from gas and power cuts. It's the same kit that serves you in a blackout.

When it isn't enough. It's not a rescue or rubble kit: it won't turn you into a firefighter. Nor does it replace structural reinforcement, or knowing whether your building is one of those that holds up: that isn't solved with a backpack.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Running to the stairs as soon as the shaking starts. Most injuries happen while fleeing: in a stairwell, with people and falling objects. The right place is under a sturdy table.
  2. Sleeping without shoes nearby and without a flashlight at hand. A nighttime earthquake + glass-covered floor = cut feet and darkness, just when you need to get out and help.
  3. Lighting a match, turning on the light or using your mobile when you smell gas. It's the spark that causes the explosion. First shut off the gas, then everything else.
  4. Not knowing where the gas valve is or not having a tool to shut it off. It's the first thing to do and almost no one has ever touched it.
  5. Confusing “the house is still standing” with “the house is safe”. You need to check for new cracks, doors that won't close and damp before sleeping inside: aftershocks bring down what the first tremor left weakened.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Anchor shelves, the TV and tall wardrobes to the wall: the No. 1 cause of minor injuries in an earthquake is things falling off the walls
  • Find the gas and water shut-off valve and check that it turns: after the quake you'll have 30 seconds and shaking hands
  • Keep closed shoes and a flashlight next to the bed (under the bed, tied together): at night, the things that cut most are shards of glass on the floor
  • Agree on a meeting point outside the building and a contact outside the province: local lines get overloaded, outside ones don't
  • Keep the backpack near the exit door, not in the basement storage room

→ During

  • Drop, take cover under a sturdy table and hold on to it: the table moves with you and protects you from falling objects
  • Don't run for the stairs or use the elevator: most injuries happen while fleeing and in stairwells
  • Stay away from windows, mirrors, shelves and building facades. If you are outside, go to an open space away from overhanging ledges and cables
  • If you are in a car, stop in an open area, not under a bridge, streetlights or power lines
  • If you smell gas, don't turn anything on: no lights, no matches, and no mobile phone near a leak

→ After

  • Put on closed-toe shoes before walking around the house: broken glass is the leading cause of injuries and you can't see it in the dark
  • Shut off gas and water if there is a smell or damage, and don't turn on switches if you suspect a leak
  • Wait and listen to the radio: aftershocks are the norm in the following hours and instructions come via radio stations and ES-Alert
  • Don't go into damaged buildings or use the elevator; check the structure before staying (new cracks, doors that won't close)
  • Record damage with photos and report it to your insurance company and town council: aid is allocated by declared areas, and that requires documentation
