Who it's for. For anyone who wants to be covered without having to leave home: water outages, lockdowns, storms that isolate a neighborhood, or simply a week when you don't feel like or can't go out to buy supplies. It's the pantry stock, not the backpack.

When it isn't enough. It's not for evacuation: if you have to leave in 15 minutes, this stays where it is. In that case, prepare the evacuation backpack with the essentials. And if you have a pet or baby, the reserve must include their supplies from day one, not when they run out.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Buying things nobody eats at home. An emergency pantry is rotated by eating it, and if you don't like it, it doesn't get rotated.
  2. Not having a can opener: if you have 30 cans and can't open them, you don't have food, you have a gym weight.
  3. Not having a way to cook without electricity: twenty cans and an unlit stove.
  4. Forgetting water for use: without a tap you can't flush the toilet. Filling the bathtub in time is free.
  5. Storing everything together in a single cupboard: if one room floods or gets damaged, you lose the entire reserve.
  6. Not adding the household's chronic medication to the plan: two weeks' reserve of treatment is the difference between sheltering in place and having to go out.
  7. Using the phone as a flashlight: the most expensive light is the one that leaves you without battery to call
  8. Storing batteries inside the radio or flashlight: they corrode and ruin the device just when you need it. Keep them separate, in their packaging

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Calculate water and food for everyone in the household × 3 days
  • If there are children, babies or pets: tailor the kit to them
  • Keep games or books on hand to keep morale up
  • Keep a manual can opener and a tested butane stove: without them, your canned food is useless
  • Fill at least one bathtub with water for non-drinking use (toilet cisterns, cleaning) if the outage is imminent
  • Test the radio, flashlight and stove once a quarter and keep spare batteries of the same size: a kit with dead batteries is not a kit
  • Charge the power bank and check expiry dates every six months, on the same day you change the clocks: that way you'll never forget

→ During

  • Ration water if the supply is cut off
  • Ventilate daily if possible
  • Keep the official radio on at least twice a day
  • Open windows for 5 minutes every few hours to refresh the air: accumulated CO2 makes you sad and slow
  • If you light a butane stove, candle or brazier, do it with ventilation and with the CO detector on: indoors, combustion consumes oxygen and carbon monoxide gives no warning
  • Phone in power-saving mode and with data off; call when you have coverage and save the powerbank for the radio and flashlight

→ After

  • Inventory of what has been used and immediate replacement
  • Replace batteries, water and food consumed the same day, not the following week: the next emergency gives no warning
  • Write down what you missed during those days: that list, and no other, is your shopping list for the kit
