The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Not having BACKUP FORMULA: milk supply drops under stress and without 2-3 days of formula, that doubles the anxiety. Even if you breastfeed, put 400-800 g of formula on the list.
  2. Relying on always being able to boil water: without gas or electricity you can't sterilize bottles — you need a dedicated safe-water bag and a cup, not just a bottle.
  3. Not counting diapers: a baby uses 6-8 diapers a day. 72 hours is ~20 diapers: a small bag that almost nobody packs.
  4. Storing the new puree that the baby has never tried: in an emergency you try food you already know they tolerate.
  5. Forgetting the 'next size up' coat: babies grow and the coat set in the kit becomes too small at 3 months without anyone noticing.
  6. Storing the bottle the baby didn't finish for the next feed. With powdered formula you throw away what's left: it's the most common mistake in an emergency and the one that causes the most gastroenteritis in a baby.
  7. Leaving liquid formula in the car trunk or storage room: heat degrades it before the expiry date. Powder, however, does not go in the fridge (humidity makes it clump): cool and dry, not cold.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Write down the baby's medication schedule/feeding plan and your pediatrician's phone number on a piece of paper: in an emergency, not knowing what they are taking is a problem
  • Keep a bottle and a sippy cup that you never use: they are spares, so they are not in the dishwasher the day you need them
  • Store photocopies of the baby's ID/health book and vaccination record in the waterproof pouch
  • Talk to your family: who takes the baby if you have to evacuate quickly? Decide roles now
  • Write the opening date on the lid of the formula can and set a reminder for 25 days: once opened it is used within about 4 weeks (as stated by the manufacturer), it does not last until the printed expiry date. Rotating it is what makes the kit useful.

→ During

  • With a baby in your arms, the evacuation backpack is split: someone carries the child, you carry the little one's 24-hour bag
  • If you don't know whether you will be able to breastfeed calmly, express milk with a manual pump in the first days so you don't lose supply
  • Watch the baby's temperature: without power there is no room thermometer, feeling the back of the neck and chest is misleading, use the thermometer from the first-aid kit
  • Doubtful water for the bottle: boiling is not always possible — treat it and let it cool, or use low-mineral bottled water kept in reserve for infant feeding
  • Powdered formula: prepare it just before the feed (AESAN recommends consuming it within 2 hours) and throw away what is left in the bottle the baby has already drunk from. With ready-to-feed liquid formula you don't have that clock.
  • With no way to boil: sterilize with cold-water sterilizing tablets and rinse with safe drinking water. And if the baby is under 28 days old, was born premature or with low birth weight, AESAN indicates boiled water and adding powder when the water is above 70 °C (check the label and ask your pediatrician).

→ After

  • Check for bumps and frights: babies cannot verbalize, any odd change in behavior or sleep, see the pediatrician
  • Restock what you use the same day: a diaper and milk supply that is not replenished is a half-finished kit
  • If the crisis was long, ask your health center to check the vaccination record and bring it up to date if needed
  • Restock with the current size and dose, not the kit's: babies change every month. And if during those days there was fever, vomiting or fewer wet diapers than usual, tell your pediatrician before considering the episode over.
