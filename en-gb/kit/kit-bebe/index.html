The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Not having RESERVE FORMULA: milk supply drops with stress and without 2-3 days' formula, that causes double the anxiety. Even if you breastfeed, put 400-800 g of formula on the list.
  2. Relying on always being able to boil water: without gas or electricity you can't sterilize bottles — you need a dedicated drinking-water bag and a cup, not just a bottle.
  3. Not counting diapers: a baby uses 6-8 diapers a day. 72 hours is ~20 diapers: a small bag that almost nobody includes.
  4. Keeping a new puree the baby has never tried: in an emergency, give food you already know they tolerate.
  5. Forgetting the 'next size up' coat: the baby grows and the coat outfit in the kit becomes too small at 3 months without anyone noticing.
  6. Keeping the bottle the baby hasn't finished for the next feed. With powdered formula you throw away what's left: it's the most common mistake in an emergency and the one that causes the most gastroenteritis in a baby.
  7. Leaving liquid formula in the trunk or storage room: heat degrades it before the expiry date. Powder, however, does not go in the fridge (moisture makes it clump): cool and dry, not cold.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Write down on paper the baby's medication/feeding schedule and your paediatrician's phone number: in an emergency, not knowing what they take is a mess
  • Keep a bottle and a training cup that you never use: they're spares, so they're not in the dishwasher on the day you need them
  • Keep photocopies of the baby's ID/health record and vaccination card in the waterproof pouch
  • Talk to your family: who takes the baby if you have to evacuate quickly? Decide roles now
  • Write the opening date on the lid of the formula tin and set a reminder for 25 days: once opened, it should be used within about 4 weeks (as stated by the manufacturer); it won't last until the printed expiry date. Rotating it is what makes the kit work.

→ During

  • With a baby in your arms, the evacuation backpack is split: someone carries the child, you carry the baby's 24-hour bag
  • If you're not sure you'll be able to breastfeed calmly, express milk with a manual pump in the first few days so you don't lose your supply
  • Watch the baby's temperature: without power there's no room thermometer, feeling the back of the neck and chest is misleading, use the thermometer from the first-aid kit
  • Doubtful water for the bottle: boiling isn't always possible — purify and cool, or use low-mineral bottled water kept in reserve for infant feeding
  • Powdered formula: prepare it just before feeding (AESAN recommends using it within 2 hours) and throw away any leftover from a bottle the baby has already fed from. With ready-to-use liquid formula, you don't have that clock.
  • If you can't boil: sterilize with cold-water sterilizing tablets and rinse with drinking water. And if the baby is under 28 days old, was born premature or with low birth weight, AESAN says to use boiled water and add the powder to water above 70 °C (check the label and with your pediatrician).

→ After

  • Check for bumps and knocks: babies can't tell you, so any unusual change in behavior or sleep means see the pediatrician
  • Restock what was used the same day: a diaper and milk reserve that isn't replenished is a half-finished kit
  • If the crisis was long, ask your health center to check your vaccination card and bring it up to date if needed
  • Restock with the current size and dose, not those in the kit: the baby changes every month. And if during those days there was fever, vomiting or fewer wet diapers than normal, tell your pediatrician before considering the episode over.
