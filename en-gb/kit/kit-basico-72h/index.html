Who it's for. For any household in Spain, wherever you start. It is the resilience bag the EU has called for since March 2025 — water, food, light, information, documents and shelter to last 72 hours without services — with every product checked: no listings that say one thing and sell another. If you are only going to put together one kit, make it this one.

When it isn't enough. It's not a backpack for walking out, and it doesn't replace the specific kit for your hazard (DANA, mountains, car). If you live in a flood-prone area or with dependent people, complete it with the evacuation kit and the baby kit or the elderly person's kit.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Buying without checking the actual product page: half of the online 'essentials' lists link to a women's raincoat, a decorative poster instead of a map, or tablets for 200-litre tanks. Before you pay, check that the photo, gender/size and dosage unit are the ones you need.
  2. Buying only bottled water and having no plan B: if it runs out, purification tablets for 10 € give you treated tap or rainwater.
  3. Storing everything in the storage room or the top cupboard. In a blackout with no light you need to find it in 30 seconds, better next to the door.
  4. Not writing the purchase date on each pack: after two years you won't know what's old and you'll throw away half your supplies.
  5. Forgetting the radio. In a long crisis your phone goes silent; the battery-powered radio is your only link to what Protección Civil orders.
  6. Buying everything all at once and then letting it sit. It works better to add two or three items each week with your shopping.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Put the kit together with the whole family and keep it in a known, accessible place
  • Check expiry dates (water, food, batteries, medication) every 6 months
  • Write emergency numbers on paper: 112, your local Protección Civil
  • Turn on mobile alerts in your phone settings: the ES-Alert system (National Alert Network) sends warnings directly to people in the affected area. The Basic Guidelines approved in April 2026 incorporate it into all civil protection plans

→ During

  • Follow only instructions from official sources (112, Protección Civil)
  • Ration water and phone battery
  • Keep the battery-powered radio on in the FM band

→ After

  • Replace what you've used as soon as possible
  • Note down what was missing and adjust the kit
