Who it's for. For those who want the next step: stop depending on the shopping cart and understand where the food they eat comes from. Starting with a balcony of sprouts is realistic, cheap and hooks you. It is also your food insurance: a few kilos of well-stored seeds provide food for a year.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Starting with 20 varieties and a big garden. It gets abandoned in six weeks. Start with three things you actually eat: tomato, lettuce, herbs.
  2. Sowing when the calendar says so instead of when your climate says so: sow when your valley's temperature allows it, not when the table says so.
  3. Using cheap growing medium from the discount store or garden soil in pots: it compacts, doesn't drain and you kill the roots.
  4. Not having water secured: in a balcony garden, a weekend away without watering is a dead garden.
  5. Storing seeds without drying them and without a date: after two years nothing germinates. Airtight jar, label, cool dark place.
  6. Throwing kitchen scraps in the bin: a 20 L compost bin costs less than 35 € and turns that waste into free fertilizer. The right ratio is two parts browns (dry leaves, cardboard) to one part greens (food scraps).
  7. Relying on insecticides without netting: whitefly reproduces in two weeks and insecticides kill bees. Cover first, treat afterwards.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Observe your space: how many hours of direct sun it gets per day (4 h minimum for tomatoes, 2-3 h is enough for lettuce and herbs)
  • Start with what is easy and productive: lettuce, arugula, chard, herbs, radishes
  • Prepare the calendar: in Spain, spring sowing runs from February to April; autumn for spinach, garlic and onion
  • Set up a household compost bin: kitchen scraps feed the garden. A 20 L bucket with a tap costs €25-35 and prevents odors
  • Cover windows with anti-aphid netting (2x8 m, €10-20): without protection, whiteflies and thrips destroy a balcony garden in two weeks
  • Check your homeowners' association rules or housing portal: some buildings (especially rentals) prohibit pots on balconies or communal terraces. If they allow them, ask about maximum weight and watering.
  • Buy pots with a saucer: the saucer catches the irrigation water and prevents stains on the floor of the entrance or shared terrace. Without a saucer, nobody will let you keep the garden.

→ During

  • Water early in the morning or at dusk, never in direct sun
  • Loose, well-draining growing medium: a pot without holes is certain death for any plant
  • Feed every 2-3 weeks with compost or liquid fertilizer
  • If you use drip irrigation, check the drippers every week: they clog easily and a dry plant goes unnoticed until it's too late
  • Check the aphid netting after storms: if it's torn, replace it. A 2 cm hole is enough for whitefly to get in
  • If you grow in pots, renew the growing medium every season: after one season it has lost nutrients and become compacted. About 5-8 € per pot and two new harvests.

→ After

  • Harvest from the outside (outer leaves) so the plant keeps producing
  • Save seeds from your best plants for the next cycle
  • Note down what worked and what didn't: your garden notebook is your best tool
