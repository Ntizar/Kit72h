The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Leaving it in the storage room or in the car: if you are evacuating at night from your bed, the backpack has to be in the hallway, hanging up, with everything inside.
  2. Packing clothes for a month. It weighs 12 kilos, your shoulders hurt at 4 km and you end up leaving things behind along the way. 30-40 L and under 8 kg.
  3. Not testing it: walk 5 kilometres with it one Sunday. It's the only way to know what you're missing and what you don't need.
  4. Not including spare keys, documents and a rechargeable phone card that doesn't depend on your own line.
  5. Forgetting small cash and a charger: in the first days, the queue to recharge your phone is the most repeated scene in any disaster.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Backpack in the hallway, always in the same place
  • Copy of documents in a waterproof bag
  • Evacuation route agreed with the family
  • Spare house keys in the backpack: in a panic it's the first thing people forget
  • Test the backpack by walking 5 km with everything inside: that's the only way to know whether it carries well and what you're missing

→ During

  • Don't go back home for things you forgot
  • Follow only the instructions of the emergency services
  • If you have to move on foot, wear closed, comfortable shoes: the ground may be covered in glass and debris
  • If you evacuate at night or with poor visibility along an interurban road, wear the reflective item: from 1/10/2026 it is mandatory for pedestrians on the carriageway or hard shoulder (RD 518/2026, art. 123, BOE-A-2026-13889)

→ After

  • Do not enter your home until you are officially authorised
  • Always wear protective footwear and gloves when handling debris: mud and debris after a flood or fire can be contaminated
  • Check the backpack every 6 months: update expired medication, food and documents
