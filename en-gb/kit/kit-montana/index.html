The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Going out in city clothes and trusting the weather app. In the mountains, the temperature drops 10 degrees going up a ravine, and fog can arrive in 20 minutes.
  2. A single flashlight for the group, with no spare batteries, at the bottom of the backpack.
  3. Not carrying an extra warm layer “because I'm doing the route in two hours”: half an hour waiting for a rescue in a gully is spent at zero degrees.
  4. Drinking little and arriving at the end dehydrated and with heatstroke, especially in spring with sun and a backpack.
  5. Relying on your phone to navigate. It runs out of battery, gets dropped, gets wet. Map and compass, and know how to use them before you need them.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Always share your route and return time with someone
  • Check the weather warning (AEMET) before you set out
  • Download the 112 app for your region
  • Never wear new footwear or socks for the first time on a long hike: 74% of pilgrims on the Camino de Santiago get blisters in the first few days (Colexio de Podólogos de Galicia)

→ During

  • If you get lost, stay put and make yourself visible
  • Ration water and don't stray from the known route
  • Use trekking poles if possible: they reduce impact on knees and ankles by up to 30%
  • If you feel rubbing: stop and apply a hydrocolloid dressing before the blister forms (Compeed, Scholl or similar)

→ After

  • Restock and check the kit after every outing
  • Note down what you were missing for next time
  • Dry and store the rain jacket: moisture leads to mold and it loses its waterproofing
