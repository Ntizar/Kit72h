The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Staying to defend the house when the fire is already close. Houses are defended beforehand (by clearing vegetation and wetting), not when the front is 200 meters away: from that point on, you only evacuate.
  2. Taking the car towards the hills «because it is closer». The fire climbs the slope and cuts off the upper road first. The emergency exit is almost always downhill and downwind.
  3. Wearing synthetic clothing and sandals. That is the combination that turns a touch of heat into a burn fused to the skin and makes evacuation on foot impossible.
  4. Shouting to warn someone far away. Smoke leaves you without air within minutes: use a whistle and call, never run or shout.
  5. Believing the danger ends when the fire front is out. Embers reignite with the wind, and burned trees and ground remain dangerous for hours afterwards.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Clear 10 metres around the house: scrub, firewood, dry grass and hedges against the façade are the fuel that carries fire to your windows
  • Have metal sheets or wooden shutters to cover windows, and keep the backpack by the entrance, not at the back of the garage
  • Note down the municipal evacuation plan and the alternative route: in a fire, the road going up into the hills is the first to be closed
  • Mentally walk the exit at night: if a warning wakes you at 3:00 with smoke, you won't be able to improvise
  • Keep work clothes and closed-toe footwear within reach: synthetic clothing melts onto the skin

→ During

  • Evacuate as soon as there is an order or smoke in the air, carrying only what is essential: a car is faster than a defended house
  • Close windows, doors and blinds to reduce drafts, and turn off ventilation and heating before leaving
  • If you are caught in the car: do not get out; wait with the windows closed, lights on and air on recirculation; the car offers more protection than running
  • If you are caught on foot: always move downhill and downwind (fire climbs the slope faster than you can), look for a clear area with no vegetation and, as a last resort, lie down in a place that has already burned
  • Cover your nose and mouth with a damp cloth and do not breathe through your mouth; smoke is more dangerous than the first line of flames

→ After

  • Do not return until the authorities allow it: burned ground still has hot spots and roots are burning underground
  • When you arrive, do not turn anything on until you have checked the installations: the service line and meter are often damaged
  • Wet the outside and check the perimeter for hours: embers reignite with the wind
  • Report it if you suspect the origin and keep photos of the damage for the insurance and the local council
