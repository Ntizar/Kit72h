Best portable solar kit for home: 5 models compared (2026)

A portable solar kit for home is a power station —a stationary battery with inverter and AC/USB ports— to which one or more flexible solar panels can be connected. The result is a system that charges your battery with the sun and delivers clean electricity, with no noise and no fumes. Unlike a petrol generator, you don't need to store fuel and you can leave it plugged in for days without supervision.

What is it useful for at home? Keeping the fridge, fibre router, mobile phone and lights on during a power outage lasting hours or a couple of days. It won't power an entire home (heating, kitchen appliances or the pool pump), but it will power what you really need when the power goes out.

I have tested and compared the five best-selling models in Spain in the 500-1000 Wh category (the one that fits in a suitcase, not a truck). The table at the end sums it all up at a glance.

How to choose your solar power station: what really matters

Before looking at brands, there are three numbers that make the difference between a kit that gets you through the blackout and one that doesn't even last until midday.

Real capacity (Wh): how many charges it gives you

Wh (watt-hours) measure how much “fuel” the battery has. A 500 Wh power station can charge a 60 Wh laptop about 6-7 times (conversion losses included), or keep a 15 W router on for 25-30 hours. With 1000 Wh, you double that.

The practical formula: kit Wh ÷ appliance consumption in W = hours of runtime. For a household that wants to keep the fridge (100-200 W) + router (15 W) + lights (30 W), the combined load is around 150-250 W. A 500 Wh power station would give about 2-3 hours of fridge time (the fridge opens and closes from time to time, so in practice a little more), while a 1000 Wh one would give 4-6 hours.

For 72 hours of off-grid runtime, you need something more: either a 1500-2000 Wh power station with panels that charge during the day, or a petrol generator as backup for nights without sun.

Output power (W): what you can plug in at the same time

Output watts indicate how many devices you can have on at once. A 300-500 W model is enough for a phone, tablet, router and lights; an 800-1000 W one is also enough for a small fridge, a tool charger or a small TV. If you want to keep a large combi fridge running (300-400 W startup), you need at least 1000 W continuous and 2000 W peak.

Included panels: how many W and how they fold

The panel is what recharges the battery during the day. A 100 W panel produces about 60-80 Wh in real terms (losses due to temperature, angle and cloud cover) in Spain on a spring-summer day; in winter, half that. The ideal is a kit that includes at least one 60-100 W panel and allows you to add another one in series.

Solar panels: how much they produce in Spain

A 100 W panel in Spain produces, on annual average, between 150 and 200 Wh per day (1.5-2 kWh), depending on orientation and time of year. In winter (December-January), irradiance drops by 40-50%: the same panel delivers 80-120 Wh. In summer, it can exceed 250 Wh.

This means that with a 100 W panel you won't recharge a 1000 Wh power station on a cloudy winter day. The key is to have panels that add up to at least twice what you consume per day, and to orient them south without shade.

Yes, you can also use the panels with the blackout kit as a complement, but for main use you need more than a panel hanging in the balcony window.

Portable solar or petrol generator?

They are not mutually exclusive, and in fact the combination is what most people use. Portable solar charges when there is sun (zero noise, no fumes); the petrol generator comes in when night approaches and solar charging is over. The generator vs power station comparison details when each one makes more sense.

If your priority is silence, not having to store petrol and not carrying it up stairs, portable solar is the right route. If you need to power a large fridge for three days without sun, combine solar + generator.

Checklist before buying

  • Capacity ≥ 500 Wh for minimum use (phone, router, lights). ≥ 1000 Wh with fridge included.
  • Output power ≥ 800 W if you want to connect a small fridge. ≥ 1500 W if you also want a microwave or tools.
  • LFP battery (lithium iron phosphate) if you're going to use it long term: 3000+ cycles vs 500-1000 of older lithium.
  • Included panels or at least compatible ones (MC4 or XT60 connectors).
  • Minimum 2-year warranty with technical service in Spain.
  • Weight and dimensions: make sure you can carry it up the stairs if you live on a high floor.
  • Monitoring app: knowing how much battery is left and how many W you're consuming at any moment is essential.

Common mistakes

  • Buying only the power station without panels. The battery drains in a couple of days off-grid and, if you don't have panels, it won't recharge until the power comes back. Always buy the kit with at least one panel.
  • Pointing the panel north or into the shade. A panel in partial shade loses up to 80% of its output. Always point it south, with no obstacles.
  • Underestimating fridge consumption. A fridge consumes 100-200 W on average but when it starts it draws 300-900 W peak. If the output power isn't enough, the fridge won't start and will cut out.
  • Forgetting the connection cables. Panels have MC4 connectors that sometimes don't fit directly into the power station. Check beforehand or carry adapters.
  • Thinking it will power everything. A portable power station is not a construction generator. Turn off heating, air conditioning and induction hobs; keep the essentials on and the sun will keep it running.

Verdict

For most Spanish households, the Jackery Explorer 1000 Pro is the ideal point: 1000 Wh to keep the fridge and router running, 2000 W output for whatever you plug in, included panels to start without complications and 12 kg that anyone can move. If your budget is tighter, the VTOMAN Flash 1500 gives you more Wh per euro.

If your priority is absolute runtime (72 h+ without sun), the BLUETTI AC200MAX is the most capable, although it weighs 26 kg. And if you want the most advanced model with the highest power, the Anker Solix F3800 is the ultimate beast: 3840 Wh, 3600 W and an LFP battery for years of use.

And remember: a portable solar kit is the energy piece of a blackout kit. Without water, without a first-aid kit and without a radio there is no plan. Start with the basics and add clean energy.
