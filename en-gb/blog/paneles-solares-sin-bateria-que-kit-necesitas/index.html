Solar panels without a battery: what kit do you need if your home has island mode (2026)

📅 2026-10-28 · ⏱ 7 min min read

Real question from a reader in Málaga: “What kit do you recommend for a family of 2 adults, with a detached house, 16 solar panels (no battery for storage but with a backup system to be able to use the power generated by the panels in island mode) in the Málaga area?”

It’s the best question we’ve received this month, because it describes a house that is almost prepared: plenty of solar generation and island capability, but zero storage. We’re going to answer it properly, because the answer isn’t “buy the most expensive kit”.

What you already have (and don’t need to buy)

A detached house with 16 panels and isolated export (“island mode”) has solved the hardest problem of a blackout in an urban home: some electricity of your own while the sun is shining. Most people preparing an emergency kit start from zero; this house starts with an advantage. Málaga is also one of the areas with the most sunshine hours in Europe, so daytime generation isn’t the bottleneck.

What the house already covers: fridge and freezer during daylight hours, LED lighting during the day, charging phones and laptops, and even some appliances used occasionally at midday.

The real gap: night and grey days

Without a battery, the system isolates load from generation: instantaneous consumption has to come from the panels. So the weak point appears:

  • At night: no sun, no power from the panels. The house depends on the grid again… and in a blackout the grid isn’t there.
  • On cloudy or stormy days: generation drops by 70-90%. If the outage coincides with a storm (the October 2024 DANA in the Valencian Community, for example), you have minimal generation just when you need it most.
  • At consumption peaks: a hob, an oven or an irrigation pump can exceed the instantaneous power available in island mode.

And watch out: island mode has its own risk. If the grid comes back and the backup system doesn’t switch properly, it can feed energy back into the grid (danger for the technicians repairing it) or disagree with the meter. Anti-islanding protection is mandatory and is checked in an inspection; if you haven’t had the installer review it for years, now is the time.

The recommendation: four layers, in this order

1. Blackout Kit — the non-negotiable base (~90-200 €)

No matter how good the solar installation is: on 28 April 2025 the Iberian Peninsula went more than 12 hours without power and homes with their own isolated generation fared best… the ones that also had the basics. Complete Blackout Kit (90-200 €): battery-powered LED lamps with magnets, head torches, battery radio, CO detector, gas hob with cylinders, large power bank and freezer packs for the fridge. With daytime generation covering the fridge and device charging, this base is inexpensive and very effective.

2. Portable solar storage — fill the night-time gap (400-1.200 €)

Here is the highest-impact improvement: a power station (portable power station, 500-1.000 Wh, LiFePO4). It isn’t a home battery: it’s a standalone unit that you charge with your own panels during sunny hours and that gives you light, fridge and electronics at night. Compare options in Best power station for home blackouts (2026) and Best portable solar kit for home (2026). Since the house already has panels, the power station doesn’t need to include a panel: it can be charged from the system itself in island mode (or with the kit’s folding panel if the system isolates the load).

3. Electricity Kit — complete the energy environment (100-500 €)

Electricity Kit: 60-100 W folding backup panel, 20.000-27.000 mAh power banks with AC or USB-C PD output, rechargeable LED area light, head torches, surge-protected power strip and plenty of AA/AAA batteries. It’s the domestic complement to the solar kit: the house has “large” generation during the day, but when you need to move energy point to point (charge a phone from the power station, light a room with the area lamp), this kit provides the flexibility.

4. Risks specific to Málaga: heat, torrential rain and fires

The Málaga area combines three risks that the general kit doesn’t fully cover:

  • Heatwave: the natural risk that causes the most deaths in Spain. Heat Kit (30-70 €): USB fan, insulated bottle, rehydration salts, cooling towels. In July and August, with no air conditioning if the grid goes down, this is what prevents heatstroke.
  • Cold drops and torrential rain: the Costa del Sol has DANA-type episodes with localised flooding. DANA Kit (90-160 €): drainage pumps, moisture protections, sandbags and barriers. A detached house has more exposed surface than a flat.
  • Peri-urban fire: if the house sits between urban area and hills, Fire Kit (60-140 €) and the evacuation plan.

What you don’t need to buy

  • Fuel generator: with 16 panels and island mode, a generator adds little except for tools or if there are large mandatory loads. It means noise, maintenance, fuel that expires and community regulations. Rule it out.
  • Professional Kit (2.000-5.000 €): it makes sense for someone starting from zero on energy. This house already has generation sorted; the gap is only night-time storage, which the power station covers for 400-1.200 €.
  • Second large home battery: if you eventually want fixed storage, that’s a separate job. With the portable power station you cover 80% of the emergency scenario for a fraction of the cost.

The 3-step plan

  1. This week: put together the 72h Basic Kit for 2 people (100-180 €) — the water and food reserve for 72 h that the EU requires as a minimum.
  2. This month: add the Blackout Kit and the power station. Test your backup’s island mode one afternoon: switch off the main breaker, let the system isolate the load, and check what stays on with and without the grid. What you don’t test doesn’t work.
  3. This quarter: full Electricity Kit + local risk kits (Heat, DANA, Fire) depending on the exact surroundings of the property.

Frequently asked questions

With 16 panels and island mode, do I need a fixed home battery?

For emergencies, no. A 500-1.000 Wh power station charged with your own panels covers the night (light, fridge, communications) for 400-1.200 €. A fixed home battery is a comfort upgrade for day-to-day life, not a preparedness requirement.

Can I charge the power station from my roof panels in island mode?

It depends on the backup system: many hybrid inverters allow an auxiliary circuit to be dedicated to charging the portable battery during the day. Check with your installer that island mode leaves energy available on the circuit where you plug in the station; if not, the Electricity Kit’s folding panel is the plan B.

What if the blackout lasts more than 72 hours?

The 72h Basic Kit is the European minimum; for 2 people with a detached house, the 30-Day Kit (300-500 €) adds an extra week of water, food and gas without relying on the grid. With the house’s solar generation, hot water and cooked food are covered during the day.
