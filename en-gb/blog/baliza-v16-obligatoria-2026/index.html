V16 beacon mandatory in 2026: rules, 80 € fine and how to choose it

📅 2026-10-11 · ⏱ 6 min min read

1 January 2026 has already passed, and with it a twenty-six-year-old habit: warning triangles are no longer a valid way to signal a stopped car on the road. From that date, the only legal device is the connected V-16 light beacon, and anyone who still relies on the triangles in the trunk is outside the rules — it is a minor offence and carries an €80 fine.

This is not a technological whim: the DGT estimates that around 25 people die each year after being struck on the road having got out of their vehicle, many while they were placing the triangles. With the V-16 you do not get out of the car: it is switched on from inside and, as well as the light, it sends the vehicle's position to other drivers. Here you can see what the rules require, how it works, where to place it, the fine, the myths and what to check before buying.

What the rules require from 1 January 2026

The obligation is set out in Annex XII of the General Vehicle Regulations and came into force on 1 January 2026: from that date, the connected V-16 is “the only legal means of signalling immobilised vehicles”, according to the DGT.

  • Compulsory for passenger cars, vans, buses, mixed-use vehicles, trucks and non-special vehicle combinations. It is not compulsory for motorcycles, but the DGT considers its use “highly advisable”.
  • Warning triangles may remain in the vehicle's equipment, but they can no longer be used to signal a breakdown on the road.
  • Abroad, it is valid in the countries that signed the Vienna Convention (Portugal, France, Germany, Italy…), which accept the device from the country of registration.
  • And a current note: from 1 October 2026, motorcycles must also carry a high-visibility reflective vest as part of their compulsory equipment (RD 518/2026). The beacon does not replace the vest.

Why it changed: the calculation nobody wanted to make

Signalling with a triangle forces you to walk at least 100 metres along the roadway with traffic passing beside you; the United Kingdom even banned them on high-speed roads for that reason. The V-16 tackles both fronts: it is activated without leaving the cabin and adds virtual visibility to the physical light — the location of the stopped vehicle appears in navigation systems, mobility apps and variable message signs, so that drivers brake before they see you.

How it works: no mobile phone, no app and no subscription

  • Self-contained: built-in, non-removable GPS chip and SIM card. No phone, no apps, no pairing: switch it on and that's it.
  • Connectivity included in the price, with a minimum of 12 years guaranteed by the rules and no surcharge from the operators.
  • When activated, it sends the exact location to the DGT 3.0 platform, which distributes it to navigation systems, apps and signs. Without a data network, the light still works.
  • It does not call 112 or your insurer: it only signals and sends a position. You make the call.
  • Then Article 130.3 of the General Traffic Regulations applies: if the vehicle cannot resume its journey and there is a safe place outside, you get out on the side facing away from traffic; if it is not safe, you stay in the cabin with your seat belt fastened.

Where to keep it and how to place it

Three DGT rules that are always forgotten: keep it charged in the glove compartment or another accessible place in the vehicle — not in a closed trunk or at home; switch it on and place it from inside, on the highest part of the vehicle; and in buses or trucks, where the roof is out of reach, on the driver's door. Activate it once at home to check that it works and to know where it goes on the day you need it.

Before buying: certification is non-negotiable

There are more than two hundred models on the market, and the DGT is blunt: only the one that appears on its official list of certified devices is valid, all certified by IDIADA or LCOE, with the laboratory and certificate number engraved on the lens.

  • Check the DGT list before paying, not the photos in the listing: it is the only check that saves you from a surprise.
  • Be wary of subscriptions: if they ask for a monthly fee for it to “connect”, it is not the model required by the rules.
  • No registration with your details: no app and no sign-up in your name; when you buy it, the beacon is not linked to any person or number plate.
  • Where to buy it: certified connected V-16 beacon (the one in the car emergency kit), compare certified V-16 beacon models or, if you do not have one yet, reflective vest.

The fine: a minor offence and €80

Not carrying the V-16 beacon is a minor offence punishable with an €80 fine, the same amount that used to apply to anyone driving without warning triangles. That is the easy part: the real cost of going without it is standing on a road shoulder at night waiting for someone to see you. That is why the car emergency kit marks it as essential: it costs about as much as two dinners and is bought once.

Three myths circulating on social media

  1. “It tracks your movements.” It only transmits when activated, and what it sends is the location plus a technical identifier of the device itself: the AEPD confirms that there is no record linking the beacon to the identity of the person using it or to the number plate, and the rules prohibit additional functions.
  2. “You have to register it with your ID or install an app.” False: it does not need an application, a phone or personal details when you buy it.
  3. “It calls 112 on its own.” Also false. If there are injuries or the situation is serious, you call 112 and, if appropriate, your insurer's assistance service.

Ten-minute checklist for this week

  1. Check that your beacon appears on the official DGT list and that the certificate number is engraved on the lens.
  2. Charge it and leave it in the glove compartment; test it once and put it back charged.
  3. Keep the vest within reach — not under the luggage in the trunk — and your phone charged.
  4. Agree at home who gets out and on which side, or whether you simply stay inside with your seat belts fastened.
  5. Check the rest of the car kit: first-aid kit, flashlight, water and power bank, whatever keeps that call to 112 alive.
