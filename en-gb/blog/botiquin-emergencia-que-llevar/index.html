Emergency first-aid kit: what to actually pack (and what is unnecessary) (2026)

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 5 min min read

The emergency first-aid kit is not the one with the most items

A useful first-aid kit is not the one packed with 200 pharmacy items: it is the one that has what your family actually uses, in sufficient quantity, in date and at hand when an emergency strikes. Most generic supermarket pharmacy kits contain the basics and fall short in real emergencies — from a deep cut to an allergic reaction or a fever that will not go down.

In Spain, according to data from the Spanish Ministry of Health, more than 60% of calls to 112 are for non-urgent problems that could be resolved with a well-stocked first-aid kit. In real emergencies — blackouts, floods, snowstorms — the first-aid kit is the first thing people look for.

Medication: what makes the difference

Plasters alone are not enough. An emergency without painkillers is unnecessary suffering. According to the Spanish Society of Community Pharmacy, self-medication is the first line of action in 78% of minor cases. The essentials:

  • Paracetamol (tablets or children's syrup): fever, pain. Dose by weight, never above 4 g/day in adults.
  • Ibuprofen (400 mg): inflammation + pain (sprains, wound inflammation). Do not take on an empty stomach.
  • Loratadine or cetirizine (tablets): allergic reactions, hives, bites and stings. One tablet a day.
  • Oral rehydration salts (sachets): diarrhoea, vomiting, dehydration. Essential in DANA or floods where tap water may be contaminated.
  • Antacids (compound): stress gastritis, poor diet during an emergency.
  • Antibiotic ointment (Fresomicina): prevent infection in small wounds.

⚠️ Important: if anyone in the family takes chronic medication (diabetes, hypertension, thyroid), include at least 7 days of extra stock. Ask your doctor about the right amount.

What is unnecessary (and takes up space)

The ideal first-aid kit stays light. Avoid these unnecessary items:

  • Digital thermometer (it is expensive and fragile — carry an analogue mercury one; it costs 5 € and does not break)
  • Instant ice packs (they take up space and expire after 2 years — carry a small towel for cold compresses)
  • Over-the-counter antibiotics (in Spain they have NOT been sold without a prescription since 2021: it is illegal and dangerous)
  • Generic 'multi-purpose' products without a clear active ingredient
  • Pre-packaged complete supermarket first-aid kits: they usually contain the basics in insufficient quantities (2 plasters, 1 small bandage) and charge you 60% more for the pretty box

Expiry dates: check every 6 months

The Spanish Agency of Medicines (AEMPS) recommends checking expiry dates every 6 months. Expired medicines not only lose effectiveness: in some cases (such as tetracyclines) they can produce toxins. Use a mnemonic rule: when the clocks change (March and October), check the first-aid kit. Write the review date on an adhesive label inside the box.

Kit by family profile

The first-aid kit varies depending on who lives with you. Civil Protection gives these recommendations by profile:

Family with babies (0-2 years)

  • Physiological saline solution (nasal spray): 3 extra bottles for congestion and cleaning
  • Children's paracetamol (syrup) with a dosing syringe
  • Emergency diapers (5 units) in case there is no water for changing
  • Hydrogel burn dressing for minor burns

Older people

  • Copy of regular medication (at least 7 days of extra stock)
  • Fibrin thrombin powder for wounds that do not close (pressure ulcers)
  • Glucose meter with test strips (if they are diabetic)
  • Moisturizing lip balm (older skin cracks easily)

People with allergies

  • Specific antihistamine (if already prescribed)
  • Anti-dust mite products for covers (prevention)
  • High-protection sunscreen (some medicines increase photosensitivity)

Pets

  • Antiseptic for minor wounds (2% chlorhexidine)
  • Non-adherent gauze to cover wounds without sticking
  • Collar to restrain an injured animal and prevent bites

The first-aid kit inside the 72h kit

The first-aid kit is not independent: it is part of the Basic 72-Hour Kit and fits perfectly into the emergency backpack. If your priority is to build the whole kit from scratch, the step-by-step guide walks you through each category.

Remember: the most important thing is not what you carry in the first-aid kit, but knowing how to use it. Practice opening the bandages, applying the antiseptic and putting on a dressing at least once a year. An emergency leaves no room for clumsiness.

How much does it cost to put together an emergency first-aid kit?

A well-stocked first-aid kit costs between 25 € and 45 € if you buy the components separately at a pharmacy or online. A pre-packaged supermarket first-aid kit can cost the same and include fewer products.

Where should the first-aid kit be stored?

In a dry place at room temperature (between 15 and 25 °C), accessible but out of reach of children. Not in the bathroom: humidity speeds up expiry. In the evacuation kit, it goes in the backpack in a waterproof compartment.

How many times a year should it be checked?

Twice a year: in March and October, along with the clock change. It is the ideal time to replace expired items and restock what has been used.
