Complete emergency first-aid kit: 15 must-have items (2026)

📅 2026-10-02 · ⏱ 2 min min read

Health is the second block of the 72h kit (after water) and the one with the most variations depending on your family. Here is the universal base list and the adaptations for special cases.

The base first aid kit: 15 non-negotiable items

  1. Sterile gauze (pack of 4): To cover wounds without fibers getting in.
  2. Elastic bandages (3 units, 8 cm): To immobilize or hold gauze in place.
  3. Adhesive bandages (assorted sizes, 20 units): For small wounds and scratches.
  4. Antiseptic in single-dose units (10 units): Chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine. Alcohol-free.
  5. Blunt-tipped scissors: To cut bandages or clothing if necessary.
  6. Tweezers: To remove splinters or debris from a wound.
  7. Paracetamol (500 mg and 1 g): Pain and fever. Always follow the package leaflet and do not combine it with other medicines without checking their summary of product characteristics.
  8. Anti-inflammatory (ibuprofen 400 mg): Inflammation and pain. Not suitable for everyone: allergies, stomach, kidney, or heart problems are contraindications. Check its summary of product characteristics (AEMPS) and confirm with a healthcare professional.
  9. Antihistamine (loratadine or cetirizine): Allergic reactions. Important if you live in an area with many plants or bees.
  10. Antidiarrheal (oralit + loperamide): Diarrhea in an emergency is much more dangerous than at home (dehydration).
  11. Antipyretics (children's paracetamol): If there are children at home.
  12. Nitrile gloves (pair): To protect yourself when treating someone.
  13. Emergency thermal blanket: Prevents hypothermia. Weighs 20 g and can save your life.
  14. Burn cream: Aloe vera gel or a specific cream. Burns are common in emergencies with improvised cooking.
  15. Family medical list: Names of regular medicines, allergies, blood type, emergency phone numbers.

Important: this list is for guidance only, not medical advice. Before taking any medicine, read the package leaflet and its summary of product characteristics (available on the AEMPS website) and, if you have doubts—pregnancy, children, chronic illnesses, or other medicines—consult a healthcare professional or pharmacist.

Adaptations by situation

With babies: add pediatric oral rehydration solution, a digital thermometer, extra pacifiers (if they are used to them), spare diapers.

With older adults: Chronic medication for 5 extra days (diabetes, hypertension, thyroid, heart). Include a manual blood pressure monitor.

With pets: External antiparasitic, veterinary collar (so they do not lick wounds), medications if chronic.

The verdict

A basic emergency first aid kit costs between 30-50€ if you buy everything new. But the most valuable part is not the kit itself: it is your family's chronic medication. If someone at home takes daily medication, those 5-7 days of reserve ARE the emergency kit.
