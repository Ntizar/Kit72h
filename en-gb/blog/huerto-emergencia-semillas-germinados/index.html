Emergency garden: seeds and sprouts to eat when everything runs out

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 7 min min read

Why a garden is part of the emergency kit

The 72-hour kit solves the short crisis: water, food, and light for the first three days. But a long crisis — a DANA that leaves you without electricity for two weeks, a snowfall that cuts off roads for a month — demands something more. That is where the emergency garden makes the difference between surviving and not depending on anyone.

It is not about producing all your food: it is about growing some live food with the bare minimum. And the fastest way to do that is not planting vegetables — it is sprouts.

Sprouts: edible food in 3 to 6 days

Sprouts are seeds that are soaked, drained, and rinsed twice a day. In 3–6 days you have fresh, live vegetables with a vitamin density higher than that of supermarket vegetables after a week in the fridge. And they don't need direct light or soil.

Setup recipe: a glass jar with a perforated lid (or cheesecloth and a rubber band) with 2–3 tablespoons of seed. Soak for 8 hours, drain, rinse morning and night. In 4–6 days you have food. A single 500 g bag of lentils produces ~2 kg of sprouts: it feeds one person for several days.

Fact: according to FAO, sprouts increase the bioavailability of vitamin C by between 200% and 800% compared with dry seed (FAO, Nutrient content of sprouts).

The minimal balcony garden (when there is sun and water)

If the crisis drags on and you have access to water (tap, containers, rain) and some light, a garden with 4 hours of sun and three pots already produces some real food. What works best in Spain:

Fast (ready in 20–40 days)

  • Radishes — from seed to table in 25–30 days, almost impossible to fail. Three medium pots = weekly harvest.
  • Lettuce and spinach — cut the outer leaves and the plant keeps producing for weeks. They can be eaten raw.
  • Chard — very hardy, two harvests a year in a Mediterranean climate.

Seasonal (summer/winter)

  • Cherry tomato and pepper — in summer, with a large pot (minimum 30 cm) and direct sun.
  • Garlic and onion — in winter, planted in a deep pot. Slow but reliable production.
  • Herbs — basil, parsley, rosemary. They don't feed you, but they turn rice and canned food into real food. They dry and last for months.

Minimum equipment: reusable sprouting trays, all-purpose potting mix, organic variety seeds with guaranteed germination, and a watering can. With that and direct sun, you start eating from your balcony. A greenhouse comes when you've been at it for seasons, not when the crisis begins.

Long-term seeds: what to store for next year

Sprouts and fast vegetables solve weeks. For months, you need to store seeds suitable for sowing the following year. The key: buy organic seeds of guaranteed variety — not supermarket ones, which are sterile or hybrids (F1) and won't germinate if you store them.

The ones that keep best (5–10 years in an airtight container, cool and dry):

  • Legumes — lentils, chickpeas, beans. Caloric food that stores for decades. The seeds also germinate, although germination viability is 2–3 years.
  • Cereals — durum wheat, millet, pearl millet. Staple food that stores well.
  • Oilseeds — sunflower, sesame. Essential fats and long storage.

Reference: the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) maintains a seed bank with vegetable varieties that guarantee germination above 80% after 5 years of storage (USDA National Plant Germplasm System).

Sprout water: food safety

The water used to rinse sprouts touches directly what will be eaten raw. In a blackout without running water, or with water from emergency containers, you need to watch out for:

  • If the water is from the tap: in Spain, tap water is safe according to the Ministry for the Ecological Transition. No need to boil it.
  • If the water is from containers or jerrycans: if it is sealed and potable, it works for rinsing. If it is collected rainwater, boil it for 3 minutes before use.
  • If the water is from a river or spring: do not use it for sprouts that will be eaten raw. You need a purifier like those mentioned in our emergency water guide.

Sprouts grow in a warm, humid environment — ideal conditions for bacteria such as E. coli or Salmonella if the water is not clean. Daily rinsing with clean water is the most important filter.

Mistakes that kill an emergency garden

  1. Starting with supermarket seeds — supermarket seeds are commercial varieties, often treated with fungicide and sterilized. They don't germinate. Always buy sowing seeds labeled as suitable for sprouting.
  2. Overwatering — mistake No. 1. Seed that rots from too much water is more common than seed that dries out. If the seed is not rinsed with clean water morning and night, it gets moldy. Clean water, never stagnant water.
  3. No light at all — sprouts without light produce pale, weak shoots. They don't need direct sun, but they do need indirect natural light. A room with a window is enough; a dark pantry is not.
  4. Buying too many varieties — start with two: watercress (fast) and lentil (protein). If they work, expand. Don't start with ten.
