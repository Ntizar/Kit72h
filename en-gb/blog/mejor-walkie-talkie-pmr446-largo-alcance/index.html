Best PMR446 walkie-talkies for emergencies (2026): comparison

When mobile networks go down and you can't alert your family, a PMR446 walkie-talkie is the only legal communication method without a licence. But not all models achieve the same: the theoretical range of 3-5 km drops to a few hundred metres in the city, and the cheapest ones lose battery within hours. In this comparison I tell you exactly which one is worth it for your emergency kit, with real range data, battery time and price.

Spanish Civil Protection recommends carrying alternative communication means to the mobile phone for emergency situations (112 Family Plan). PMR446 is the only radio you can use without a licence across the entire EU, and in a DANA or power cut lasting several hours it is your only way to coordinate with the family without depending on phone batteries.

28 April 2025 showed what preparedness guides have been repeating for years: when all mobile networks collapse at once, you are left mute. WhatsApp doesn't work, calls don't get cut off — they simply don't exist. And in a DANA or prolonged power cut, the antennas can be left without electricity for days.

What is PMR446 and why don't you need a licence?

PMR446 means "Portable Mobile Radio" and operates on the 446 MHz frequency with a maximum of 0.5 watts (500 mW) of power. It is a harmonised European standard: in any EU country you can use a PMR446 walkie-talkie without a licence, without exams, without fees. You only have to comply with the conditions of use: fixed antenna (non-removable) and maximum power of 0.5 W.

This is important: PMR446 does not compete with the amateur radio band (which requires a licence) or with professional firefighter radios. It is a tool for short-distance communications between private individuals, ideal for emergencies where the telecommunications infrastructure has collapsed.

Tips for using walkie-talkies in emergencies

Having the walkie-talkies is not enough: you have to know how to use them correctly in an emergency. These tips come directly from Civil Protection guides and the 112 Family Plan:

1. Put all walkie-talkies on the same channel before the emergency. Channel 1 is the most universal (it has no CTCSS sub-tone), so any PMR446 walkie-talkie can hear it. Set up all 16 channels and note in the family plan which channel is used for what (channel 1 = general emergency, channel 2 = meeting point).

2. The "push-to-talk" rule: to speak, you have to press the button. Just speaking is not enough. And when you finish, release the button to listen to the reply. Under the stress of an emergency, people tend to speak without pressing the button — the person on the other side does not hear you.

3. Spare batteries: carry at least two sets of rechargeable batteries per walkie-talkie. AA batteries are the easiest to find in any camping or DIY store during an emergency.

4. Test the walkie-talkies before you need them: use them every 3-4 months to check that they work. NiMH batteries lose capacity over time if they are not recharged periodically. A walkie-talkie that won't turn on in an emergency is more dangerous than not having one at all.

How many walkie-talkies do you need?

For a family of 4, the recommended minimum is 2 PMR446 walkie-talkies (one for each adult, who can hand them out to the children). If you have older children (over 10 years old), one per person is recommended: 4 walkie-talkies for a family of 4.

For groups of more than 8 people (neighbourhood communities, groups of neighbours in an emergency), it is advisable to have a central communication point (a walkie-talkie always switched on and monitored in each entrance hall or common area) and other walkie-talkies distributed among the volunteers.

The verdict

For most Spanish families, the best PMR446 walkie-talkie for emergencies is the Motorola CP136: it is the most reliable, the easiest to use and has a reasonable price. If your budget is tight, the Altel A-308 does the job at half the price.

If you think GPS is essential for coordinating the family, the Midland G10 is worth it, but be prepared to charge it every 4-6 hours if you use it with GPS active.

Important: all models in this comparison are certified PMR446 (fixed antenna, maximum 0.5 W). Radios such as the Baofeng UV-5R or any FRS-band walkie-talkie (e.g. Motorola T460) are not legal in Spain without an amateur radio licence: they exceed the permitted power, have a removable antenna or use non-harmonised frequencies. Do not put them in your kit even if you find them cheaper: you expose yourself to a penalty from the Undersecretariat of Telecommunications and to interfering with emergency services.

Remember: a walkie-talkie without batteries is not a walkie-talkie. And a walkie-talkie you have not tested in an emergency can fail you just when you need it. Buy the walkie-talkies, put them in your emergency kit and test them every few months.
