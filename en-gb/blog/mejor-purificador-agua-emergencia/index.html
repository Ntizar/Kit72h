Best water purifier for emergencies (2026): 7 filters compared

📅 2026-10-09 · ⏱ 6 min min read

After the DANA, several municipalities were left without drinking water for days while bottles ran out in shops. And in a winter blackout the problem is not just electricity: within three days a household reaches the 72 hours of self-sufficiency recommended by the European Union Preparedness Strategy, and you decide whether to drink from the tap, the river or a bottle. That is where the emergency water purifier comes in: it is not for surviving in the jungle, it is so that any cloudy water can be drunk without ending up in the emergency room.

I compare seven real options from Amazon Spain —personal filters, gravity filters for home, a bottle with a filter and tablets— with their price, their ideal use and, above all, with what each one does not do. In emergency water, the mistake is not noticed today: it is noticed at 36 hours.

What Spanish regulations require and what a filter does not solve

In Spain, tap water is regulated by Real Decreto 3/2023, which sets the technical and health criteria and quality control: if it comes out of the tap, it is fit for drinking. The problem appears when supply is cut or when turbidity rises after a storm. That is where Protección Civil takes over: water for three days and, if there are doubts about quality, boiling or disinfecting. Its Family Emergency Plan guide expressly includes “water purification tablets” and “water (for 3 days)”.

And here is the part almost no one explains when buying:

  • Membrane filters (0.1–0.2 microns, depending on the manufacturer) remove bacteria, parasites and most microplastics. They do not remove viruses, which are much smaller, nor solvents, pesticides or hydrocarbons.
  • Chlorine or chlorine dioxide tablets do cover viruses, but they leave a taste and need contact time (minutes) before drinking, and they do not remove turbidity or chemicals either.
  • Boiling is the safest and crudest method: it kills everything living, but it does not remove dirt or heavy metals and it uses gas or batteries just when you do not have them.
  • No method fixes water with chemicals (a leak, a spill, diesel in the estuary). That water cannot be made potable: discard it.

The conclusion: a filter alone is never enough. The real combo is filter + tablets + the ability to boil. And do not forget the reserve —three liters per person per day—, set out step by step in how much water you need and how to store it.

Three real uses in a Spanish household

  • After a DANA or a flood. It comes out cloudy or with a boil-water notice: filter for drinking and chlorine for surfaces. The cleaning order is in returning home after a flood.
  • Multi-day blackout. The cistern and water heater are still full, but the pump does not work. Filling jugs from the bathtub and filtering them is faster than carrying bottles upstairs. The full script is in the blackout kit.
  • Field or trail water. River, spring or camper van tank: the same filter works for the weekend. Which is exactly what most kits lack: equipment that never gets used.

The five mistakes that leave water just as it was

  1. Filtering very cloudy water directly. Dirt clogs the membrane and leaves it with no flow in minutes. First let it settle or prefilter it with a clean cloth, then filter it.
  2. Using the filter for water with chemicals (diesel, solvent, cistern treated with garden products). Nothing on this list removes it: discard the water.
  3. Buying tablets and not checking the expiry date. Tablets lose strength; keep them dry, in their closed jar, and write the date on the jar with a marker. As with pantry food: expired is not a reserve.
  4. Filtering and drinking without allowing contact time if you also use tablets: with cold water it takes several minutes. Write it on the jug label, so the thirstiest person does not decide it.
  5. Not testing it beforehand. Equipment that has never been used gets used wrong. Filter a glass of water at home and see how long it takes: in a blackout you will already know what it does.

What to buy today (and what to leave ready)

Minimum, cheap list: a three-stage filter for the home, a personal filter for each person who leaves the house, a strip of water purification tablets and a pot with a lid for boiling. That covers bacteria, viruses and the emergency route without electricity. The set fits in the basic 72-hour kit and, if you live in a flood-prone area, in the DANA and flood kit.

And an idea that saves money: the water reserve is still the first thing. A purifier does not replace the jugs, it extends them. The three-liter-per-person-per-day rule, containers and rotation in how to store water and food at home.
