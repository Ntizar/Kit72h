The 7 official resources to keep on your phone and on paper (2026)

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 2 min min read

In a crisis, the difference between acting well and acting late is having the right source at hand. And distrusting everything else: during the 2025 blackout dozens of hoaxes circulated. Save these seven channels now — in your phone's favourites and, better still, written down on paper in your emergency folder.

The 7 essentials

  • 112: emergencies throughout the EU, free, no credit needed, and it works even if your carrier has no coverage. Save it, but also know the number by heart.
  • AlertCops (official app of the Ministry of the Interior): public safety alerts and geolocated emergencies in your area. Download it now; during a crisis app stores fail too.
  • AEMET (aemet.es and its app): weather warnings by province. In a DANA, the AEMET red warning arrives hours before the water does.
  • Protección Civil (proteccioncivil.es): self-preparedness guides by risk type and, in a crisis, behavioural recommendations. It is the source from which this website's lists are drawn.
  • DGT Traffic (official app and Twitter/X): road conditions, closures and diversions in real time — essential if you have to move or evacuate.
  • Bulletin and FM radio: a battery-powered radio tuned to national and regional stations is the only medium that survives a total network collapse.
  • Your town hall and autonomous community: every municipality has its emergency plan and its channels (regional 112, municipal alerts). Find them now, not when the siren sounds.

On paper, because phones die

Write down in your folder: 112, the Protección Civil phone number for your municipality, your health centre, two contacts outside your area, and your home insurance policy number. Half a page in handwriting that works with your phone at 0%.
