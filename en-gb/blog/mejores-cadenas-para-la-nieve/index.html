Best snow chains 2026: 5 options and when the DGT requires them

📅 2026-10-20 · ⏱ 6 min min read

The same thing happens every winter: the snow warning comes, the mountains fill up, and drivers who are only going down to the petrol station discover that they don't have chains, don't know how to fit them, or that the ones they bought three years ago no longer fit their current car. Choosing well keeps you from being stranded on a mountain pass with poor visibility—and also from arriving late where you were going—and it isn't expensive: the difference between one set of chains and another is twenty or thirty euros.

The Spanish rule is simple and worth repeating: there are no fixed dates. The obligation to carry chains (or winter tyres that replace them) is set by the signage on each stretch and by the road conditions reported by the DGT, not by a calendar. Before setting off, the official procedure is to check traffic conditions on dgt.es or mapamovilidad.dgt.es, call 011 or listen to radio bulletins; and if there is an AEMET warning for the area, it is better to postpone the plan. Everything else—what to buy, how to fit them and how to store them—is in this comparison.

1. What the DGT requires, in four points

  • The sign rules, not the calendar. Where the mandatory chains sign is posted, you cannot continue without them (or without another authorized device). The official sign catalogue was updated by Royal Decree 465/2025, so some maps and manuals show different codes for the same sign: look at the sign for the stretch, not the letter of the regulation.
  • Winter tyres only replace chains if they are marked. The DGT allows driving without chains—when the sign requires them—with tyres marked with the three-peak mountain snowflake symbol (3PMSF); the M+S marking alone is not enough to replace them. They are designed for temperatures below about 7 °C.
  • Speeds and fitting position: maximum 30 km/h with metal chains and 50 km/h with textile ones; always on the driven axle (both wheels on that axle) and, if the car is four-wheel drive, at the front.
  • Driving with chains is not the same as driving normally: no sudden acceleration or hard braking, and on road without snow braking takes longer—remove them as soon as the stretch ends.

3. Winners by use case

  • Most drivers (an occasional trip to the mountains): metal chains in your size, basic ones properly adjusted. They are accepted on all roads and are the cheapest thing that really works.
  • You live in a snowy area or make several weekend trips in a row: 3PMSF winter tyres. With them you comply where the sign requires chains, and you don't spend the rest of the year with a set of chains taking up space in the boot.
  • Cold hands, in a hurry, or never fitted them before: textile socks on stretches that allow fabric (they are the fastest to fit)—and if you don't know whether your route allows them, metal chains.
  • Off-road and unpaved tracks: metal chains with a tensioner, always; gloves and a headlamp in the same bag as them.
  • Tight budget: better a basic chain in your size than a “cheap” one in another size. The following year you add the gloves and the torch.

4. How to fit them, according to the DGT itself

  • Stop in a safe place, off the carriageway and not on a slope: better to pull the car into a wide car park than to stop on a hard shoulder with poor visibility.
  • Driven axle: front if the car is four-wheel drive; rear if it is rear-wheel drive. Both wheels on that axle, never “one on each side”.
  • Fitting: rigid hoop behind the wheel, centred, outer ring, tensioner and any excess hooked up so it doesn't hit the wheel arch.
  • Drive gently, with no sudden acceleration or braking, and remove them at the end of the snowy stretch: on clean asphalt they get in the way and wear down.
  • When storing them: wash and dry them. Road salt corrodes them from the inside, and a rusty chain breaks exactly when you need it most.

5. The five most common mistakes

  • Not testing them beforehand. The first fitting in a car park with warm hands takes two minutes; on a snowy mountain pass without gloves, twenty. Do it once at home.
  • Buying by price without checking the size. The cheapest chain that won't fasten on your wheels is not a bargain: it's a trip to the petrol station so you don't even leave the car park.
  • Taking socks to a stretch that only allows metal chains. If the sign specifies it or your usual route requires it, metal chains: they are the ones that work in all cases.
  • Storing them wet or with broken elastic straps. A two-minute check at the end of the season: clean, dry and in a bag without holes.
  • Relying only on the GPS. Road conditions are checked on dgt.es and mapamovilidad.dgt.es, by calling 011 and in radio bulletins; if there is a restriction or an AEMET warning, postpone the trip, don't force it.

6. The boot kit, in six items

In addition to chains in your size: work gloves (winter ones get soaked), a torch with spare batteries, an old towel for your hands and mud, water and something to eat in case you are stranded for a couple of hours, and the blanket or extra coat for passengers.
