Emergency kit for €50: almost everything is already at home (2026)

📅 2026-09-02 · ⏱ 4 min min read

The European recommendation of 72 hours of self-sufficiency sounds like a big purchase: tactical backpack, jerrycans, military rations, radio transmitter. False. The minimum useful preparation fits in 50 euros because 80% is already in your home and you don't know it. The method has three moves: first scavenge, then buy only what is needed, finally organize. It's not stinginess: a kit bought all at once and without habit dies in a cupboard; one built from what you already use survives.

Scavenging afternoon: what you have and didn't count on

Before you go to the checkout, go around the house with a cardboard box and a notebook:

  • Water: look at this week's shopping. The bottles you drink every day are reserve as soon as you get into the habit of buying two extra and rotating them: the old one goes, the new one comes in. A family of three builds up the 27 liters for 72 hours without buying a single large container, in closed bottles and in a dark place.
  • Food: the usual pantry of a Spanish household —rice, pasta, canned pulses, tuna, fried tomato sauce, nuts, biscuits— is already an emergency diet without fire, except for anything that needs cooking. Separate what is eaten opened and without cooking: that is your kit, nothing else.
  • Light: the hiking headlamp, the flashlight in the drawer, the birthday candles, the bike light. Every home has more lights than it thinks; the problem is that they are scattered and without batteries. Collect them, group them and test them all in one afternoon.
  • Means to boil: any pan on a camping stove, or the gas hob if the electricity went and not the butane. With fire, doubtful water becomes safe and the shopping list changes size.
  • Radio: the old transistor in the drawer may have potential, but here it usually pays to buy: a new battery-powered AM/FM radio costs 12-15 euros and is the difference between knowing what is happening and trusting the building's WhatsApp group.
  • Transport: the school backpack, the gym bag, the shopping cart. A large bin bag as an outer cover works as a waterproof layer for zero euros.

The 50 euros: what order to spend them in

On top of what you have scavenged, the purchases that give you the most self-sufficiency per euro, in order:

  • Battery-powered AM/FM radio (12-15 €): information is the first thing to go in a blackout and the least improvised.
  • Batteries for the whole set (8-10 €): the models you actually use, with some in the backpack and some in the drawer at home.
  • Headlamp with its own batteries (6-8 €): it keeps your hands free, which is literally what you need when carrying a child or closing a backpack in the rain.
  • Water purification tablets (8-12 €): insurance for when the bottles run out; they take up the space of a coin and last for years.
  • First-aid kit for wound care (8-10 €): gauze, adhesive tape, antiseptic, gloves, thermometer. Medication is what your home already uses, with advice from your pharmacist.
  • Waterproof document pouch (5 €) and a USB stick (3 €): scanned papers, a copy at another address, a little cash in 10 and 20 euro notes.

If you go five euros over, cut back on brands or look in the bargain shop: you still get there. And look at what is not on the list: no military rations, no rescue knife, no walkie-talkies, no filter bottle. None of that deserves the first euro.

What is free and worth more than what you buy

  • The rotation habit: water, food and batteries are bought to be used, not to be stockpiled. Without rotation, in a year you will have an expired kit and a sense of duty fulfilled.
  • The twenty-minute conversation: decide where the kit sleeps (a known place, at hand height, on the way to the door) and who does what if you have to leave. The behavior script is published by Civil Protection in its official recommendations; that script only needs your family's names added.
  • The six-month review: one alarm on 1 January and another on 1 July. Fifteen minutes, everything checked.
  • Critical phone numbers on paper: 112, health center, pharmacy, two contacts outside the city. With the mobile at 1%, a pen saves everything.

What this method does not cover (and you should know it)

The 50-euro kit handles the classic 72 hours: a short blackout, a water cut, a storm, an overnight evacuation. It falls short in a long winter without heating, in a home with a baby, with chronic treatment or in an isolated home with no neighbors. Those scenarios call for more investment and more planning: not because the cheap kit is bad, but because the EU recommendation is a floor, not a ceiling. When it is time to grow, you grow on what you already have: money spent on method is not thrown away, it is completed.

For the step up in quality, the basic 72h kit list separates the essential from the accessory, and the blackout kit adds exactly what this budget leaves out: power and cooking without electricity.
