Pets in an emergency: evacuation plan with dogs and cats (2026)

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 6 min min read

On 29 October 2026, the power went out for hours across much of Spain. Blackouts, snowstorms, floods… they don't give warning. And in the chaos, pets are always the first silent victims. If you have a dog, a cat, a rabbit or any domestic animal, it is not an extra detail: it is a member of your family that depends on you to survive. In this guide, I explain how to put together a real evacuation plan, what to put in your pet's emergency box and what the authorities say about it.

Why pets die in emergencies

Every year in Spain, hundreds of pets die or go missing during disasters because their owners are not prepared. According to data from Protección Civil, animals abandoned during evacuations have a 70% chance of not being located in the first 48 hours. The reasons are always the same: no one took them out of the house, they did not have an up-to-date microchip, or the owner had no evacuation plan in advance.

The World Organisation for Animal Health (OIE), a reference body of the WHO, recommends that every family emergency plan explicitly include animals. And it is no coincidence: during the pandemic, animal shelters became overwhelmed because owners did not have a basic kit ready to move their pets if they had to isolate. What you don't foresee now, you pay for later with your companion's life.

Evacuation plan: the 5 steps set out by Protección Civil

Protección Civil has a clear protocol for domestic animals in emergencies. These are the steps you should follow now, not when the storm starts:

Step 1: Identify your pet

Microchipping has been mandatory for dogs and cats in Spain since 2027 (Animal Welfare Law 7/2023), but many animals still do not have one. A dog without a microchip is like a child without an ID card: you can't prove it's yours. Ask your vet to check that the chip is registered with your current details. If you change your phone number or address and don't update it, the microchip is worthless.

Step 2: Prepare an "escape kit"

In addition to the emergency box, keep a small backpack to hand with the essentials so you can leave immediately without looking for anything else. Include: a thermal blanket (for cold or shock), a pen and paper (to leave notes on your door if you evacuate), and the leash. All of it in an accessible place known to every member of the family.

Step 3: Identify a safe place

Not all emergency accommodation accepts pets. Call hotels in your area that allow animals, friends' or relatives' homes that can take them in, and find veterinary shelters in your municipality. La Aegan (Spanish Association of Giant Breeds and Canine Breeds) keeps a list of pet-friendly accommodation on its social media.

Step 4: Prepare the documents

Keep a folder with: the microchip certificate, the European pet passport (if you have a cat or dog), up-to-date vaccination records, a recent photo, and the registration certificate from the Municipal Animal Register. If you have a breed considered potentially dangerous (RCD), you need compulsory insurance and a special permit. Without those papers, the animal may be confiscated and you may be fined.

Step 5: Practice the evacuation

An animal that has never traveled in a carrier or doesn't know a leash will panic in the chaos. Practice putting your pet in the carrier with treats. Get it to respond to its name in stressful situations. Don't wait for the alarm to sound to teach it what to do.

What to do if your pet goes missing during an emergency

The first day is critical. According to the National Meteorological Institute (AEMET), during weather alerts (snow, wind, storms), the chance of an animal escaping through the gaps in a damaged house multiplies by 10. If your pet is not at home when you start looking for it:

  • Call Protección Civil immediately (112). They manage search teams and have access to the urban CCTV network.
  • Notify your vet in case someone brings it in.
  • Post on local social media with a clear photo, description and your phone number.
  • Check animal shelters in your area in person. Don't wait for them to call you.
  • Use your mobile operator's app to locate your pet if it wears a GPS device such as an AirTag or a third-party GPS chip.

Remember: 80% of lost animals are recovered within the first 72 hours. What you do on that first day makes the difference between finding it or not.

Fatal mistakes you make with your pet in emergencies

  1. Tying your pet up outside the house — if the power goes out and doors get stuck, your animal is left locked in or lost. Always inside.
  2. Leaving water in an open bowl — if the supply is cut, the water becomes contaminated. Always keep sealed bottles to hand.
  3. Forgetting the leash — a frightened dog runs. Without a leash, there's no going back.
  4. Not keeping the microchip updated — a chip without data is like an ID card without a name.
  5. Waiting for it to "pass" — emergencies don't resolve themselves. If the signal is red, act.

Your pet's plan starts today

You don't need to spend 500 € or build a bunker. With a plastic box, three days of food, the microchip certificate and a leash, you have a basic plan. The rest can be refined: the first-aid kit, the carrier, scanned documents. But the first step is to start.

And if your pet already has its kit, share this guide with your neighbors. Because in a real emergency, no one gets left behind — not even the dog from downstairs.
