Best heater for a power cut: 5 types to get through a blackout (2026)

📅 2026-10-08 · ⏱ 5 min min read

In April the blackout lasted a few hours at eighteen degrees. In January, the same outage at three in the morning is another matter: without heating a home loses degrees in a few hours, and the National Plan for preventive actions for low temperatures of the Ministry of Health says it bluntly: cold not only causes hypothermia, it also “favours traffic accidents and falls due to ice, or fires and carbon monoxide poisoning from gas stoves, braziers, etc.” That sentence sums up this article: choosing the best stove for when the power goes out is a matter of not turning the living room into a medical problem.

I compare the five types bought in Spain —butane, pellets, electric, catalytic and the no-stove option— with their cost per hour and what they need to work when the grid is down.

How much heat you need (and why heating the whole house is a trap)

A 4,000 W stove does not heat a flat: it heats its room. The strategy during a blackout is to reduce the volume —small room, closed door, people together—. Guide rule for a closed room of 15-20 m²:

  • Well insulated (thick walls, double glazing): 700-1,000 W is enough.
  • Poorly insulated or with high ceilings: it asks for 1,500-2,000 W.
  • Above 2,500-3,000 W in 15 m² is money burned and more risk.

And an idea that weighs more than watts: heating does not replace clothing. What works: clothing in layers + hot food + low stove; the breakdown is in dressing in layers in an emergency and the night plan in how to maintain body temperature without electricity.

Winner by category

  • Best for a real blackout: the butane stove with oxygen cut-off. It does not depend on anyone, heats in minutes and one cylinder lasts several nights: 4,200 W with automatic oxygen depletion cut-off (about 84 €). The cut-off prevents the stove from going out and continuing to burn in a closed room.
  • Best for a whole house: pellets, if you already have the stove and the pallet. It heats a whole floor and is cheap per hour, but almost all need a plug for the auger, fan and ignition: without power, they do not start.
  • Best combo cooking + heat: the portable butane stove (20-45 €). It is not a stove, but it heats water and food without turning on the home kitchen: in three days of outage it pays for itself with the first pot.
  • Best budget: the no-stove option. A survival thermal blanket (3-8 €), a reusable electric body warmer when the power returns and a warm sleeping bag get you through the night: less than a cylinder, and it cannot fail.

And the catalytic stove? It is the cleanest version of butane: it burns without a visible flame and gives softer heat, more pleasant in a small room. It is worth it if the person spending the day there is an older adult or a child; for a budget kit, the money is better spent on butane and a CO detector.

The serious mistake: buying an electric heater for the blackout

It is the most common mistake: a 2,000 W convector is cheap, silent and does not generate carbon monoxide, which is why it sells so well. The problem is that in a blackout there is no voltage and the electric heater becomes furniture. To make it work you would need a 1,500-2,000 W generator; what batteries can actually handle is in best power bank for a blackout and in emergency solar generator.

Data from the Spanish context: according to the CNMC Household Panel, 43.2% of households have contracted a power capacity of between 4 and 6 kW and spent an average of 47,8 € per month on electricity. With two 2,000 W convectors plugged in you are already at the limit of that capacity; in an outage that is not a bill problem: it is that there is no current to consume.

Carbon monoxide: the four rules that save the night

CO does not smell, does not sting and does not warn: initial symptoms (headache, dizziness, nausea) are confused with a cold, and cases are concentrated from October to March, just when stoves are turned on. Official recommendations are identical across all administrations:

  • Ventilate twice a day, 15 minutes each time, even if it is cold. It is the literal indication of the Ministry of Health's cold plan.
  • Turn off electric and gas stoves during the night. Also from the cold plan: sleeping with the stove on is the decision most regretted.
  • Do not use camping stoves, barbecues, braziers or the kitchen oven inside the home, and do not cover ventilation grilles. It is the recommendation of the 112 services and Civil Protection.
  • Battery-powered carbon monoxide detector on the wall, one metre from the bed and away from the ceiling. It costs between 15 and 35 €: see CO detectors. No stove in this article is complete without it.

Checklist before the first cold snap

  • Butane stove with oxygen cut-off and two spare cylinders: the second always runs out the first night.
  • CO detector with new batteries and replacement date noted.
  • Tape and plastic to seal windows: a poorly sealed room requires double the power.
  • Blankets, warm sleeping bag and thermal clothing; lighters and long matches out of children's reach.
  • Food and water that do not need fire: three-day menus without cooking.
