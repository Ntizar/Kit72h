Blackout: lessons from 28 April 2025 in Spain (2026)

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 2 min min read

On Monday, 28 April 2025, at 12:33, the power went out across the entire Iberian Peninsula. Within minutes, metro systems, traffic lights, ATMs and card terminals stopped working; mobile networks collapsed within hours. For many, it was the first time they saw firsthand why an emergency kit is recommended. These are the lessons.

1. Information is the first thing to disappear

With no mobile phone or television, the only reliable source was a battery-powered or hand-crank radio. Those who had one knew what was happening; those who didn't relied on rumors. If you buy only one thing after reading this, make it the radio.

2. Cash was king again

Without card terminals, only businesses with manual cash registers and cash could operate. Those carrying small notes could buy; those who didn't had to wait. Keep 10-20 € notes at home for 2-3 days.

3. Your phone light isn't enough

Using your phone flashlight for hours drains the battery you need for emergencies. Use a dedicated flashlight or headlamp, with spare batteries. Candles only with extreme care — they are a common cause of fires during blackouts.

4. The elevator is not a game

People were trapped for hours. If you live in a high-rise, assume that during a blackout the stairs are the way; if you have reduced mobility, have a plan agreed with neighbors.

5. Traffic lights don't work: the road belongs to the worst driver

Cities turned into a chaos of unmarked intersections. If you drive during a blackout, treat it as an intersection where traffic from the right has priority and be extra careful.

6. The wait was long

In many areas the power returned within hours, but in others it took all night. A kit designed for 72 hours comfortably covers a national blackout; the 30-day kit covers the extreme case of something like this lasting longer.
