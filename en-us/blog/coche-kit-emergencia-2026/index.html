Car and emergencies: what is mandatory in 2026 and what is advisable

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 8 min min read

The car kit is the only one you carry with you every day, and also the most regulated. Since 1 January 2026 the main rule changed —the connected V-16 beacon replaced triangles as the only legal means of signalling—, but the rule only covers part of the problem: signalling that you are stopped. It doesn't cover running out of water on a road cut off by a DANA, spending the night in the car at minus fourteen degrees after a snowfall, or a headlight failing in the middle of a six-hour traffic jam. This article reviews both halves: what the law requires and what you should add on your own account.

We've ordered it the way you would actually use it: first the mandatory items, then the prioritised emergency equipment, then the three most common scenarios in Spain (flooding, snow and breakdown) and finally the six-monthly check that keeps everything working. The full list, with links and one-click basket, is in the car kit.

What is mandatory in Spain in 2026

The DGT is clear: since 1 January 2026 the approved connected V-16 beacon is the only legal means of signalling a vehicle immobilised on the roadway. You can check that your model is certified in the DGT's official list of V-16 devices: any orange light from a DIY store won't do; it must have the type approval and the connectivity (geolocation) required by the rule.

Triangles are no longer a valid means of signalling a stop: they can stay in the trunk, but they no longer fulfil the legal function. The offence for driving without a beacon is minor and carries an 80 € fine, but the real reason for the rule is flesh and blood: the DGT estimates that around 25 people die each year after being hit by vehicles on the road after getting out of their car, many while placing triangles. With the V-16 you don't get out of the car: it is switched on from inside and, in addition to the light, sends the vehicle's position to other drivers. The details of the rule, the fine and how to choose one are in the V-16 beacon guide.

Complete the mandatory on-board items with this short list:

  • Reflective vest inside the passenger compartment, within reach before you get out (and put it on before you touch the trunk).
  • Documentation: vehicle registration, ITV card and insurance receipt.
  • Connected V-16 beacon with the battery in date (many have a non-replaceable battery: if it is expired, it is useless).

What is worth adding (by priority)

The mandatory items get you out of legal trouble; these get you out of real trouble. Ordered from most to least useful, thinking about a typical Spanish scenario: a breakdown on a motorway, a road cut off by torrential rain or a winter traffic jam.

  • Emergency thermal blanket (3-8 € approx.): a snow traffic jam or a breakdown in February becomes endless without one; it takes up less space than a notebook. Thermal blanket.
  • LED flashlight with magnet (10-25 € approx.): change a wheel at night without anyone holding anything; the magnet attaches it to the bumper. Magnet flashlight.
  • Compact first-aid kit (15-30 € approx.): gauze, bandages, antiseptic and gloves; the list of what it includes and what is unnecessary is in the emergency first-aid kit. Car first-aid kit.
  • Water and something to eat (2-5 € and 10-15 € approx.): two bottles per person and energy bars; renewed every six months. Bottled water.
  • Power bank or 12 V jump starter (25-50 € approx.): your phone is your radio, your map and your way to alert 112; a dead phone cuts off all those options. 12 V car power bank.
  • Chains or textile snow socks (30-80 € approx.) if you cross the north or the mountains in winter: first check that your car accepts the model in your size. Snow chains.
  • Tow chain or tow strap (15-30 € approx.): gets a car out of a muddy shoulder without waiting for a tow truck.

With that, the car stops being just transport and becomes the 72-hour kit you already carry with you: several of these items are exactly the same ones we recommend for home.

Scenario 1: water — never cross a ford

It is the mistake that costs the most lives in DANA events. Moving water hides the road surface: a branch, worn asphalt or an open drain are enough. And it doesn't take that much water: 20 cm already makes the vehicle hard to control and 30 cm of moving water sweeps away a passenger car.

The recommendations of Civil Protection, collected by the Ministry for the Ecological Transition, are blunt: do not cross flooded sections on foot or by car, do not park in dry ramblas or on riverbanks, and if there is a warning of intense rain do not go down into garages or basements. If water catches you while driving:

  • Do not enter: find another route, even if it is a long detour. Turning back is always cheaper than the alternative.
  • If the water rises and the car stops, get out through the windows (not the doors, which will have the water on their side) and move to higher ground. The Red Cross sums it up like this in its flood guide: if you find yourself trapped, get out of the vehicle as soon as possible.
  • Call 112 and give the exact location: road, PK, municipality and direction. In an emergency 112 is the only number, and it works throughout Spain.

If you live in an exposed area, the car can also help you evacuate in time. The full protocol, with ES-Alert warnings and document bags, is in the DANA / flood kit.

Scenario 2: cold — the snowfall that cuts off the road

In the north of the peninsula, a storm can leave a motorway cut off for hours. Spending that night in the car with the heating on is possible, but with two safety conditions: never with the engine running in a garage or enclosed space (carbon monoxide has no smell and kills in minutes) and with the exhaust pipe clear of snow so it does not become blocked. Outdoors, with the window open a finger's width, it is safe.

The thermal blanket, a hat and hot water in a thermos make those hours bearable. And before setting out in winter, check the AEMET forecast (aemet.es → Warnings): an orange or red snow warning is not decoration; it is a sign that that route may have no way out.

Scenario 3: breakdown — the five minutes that matter most

A puncture on the M-40 at six in the evening is annoying; what is dangerous is what comes next. The protocol, in order:

  1. Pull onto the hard shoulder as far as you can and turn on the V-16 from inside, before getting out.
  2. Put on the vest before opening the door.
  3. Get out on the side away from traffic, with passengers off the road (behind the barrier, not in front of it).
  4. Keep things visible: the magnet flashlight on the bumper helps others brake earlier.
  5. If you have to wait, the blanket and water in the trunk decide whether the wait is twenty minutes or four hours.

In summer the script is the same but in reverse: ventilation, water and never leave anyone (not even a dog) inside a car parked in the sun.

How to maintain the kit: two checks a year

The car kit doesn't fail for lack of things; it fails because of expired items. The most convenient check is at the clock change, twice a year:

  • Water renewed (it should be changed every six months; if it has spent the summer in the trunk, you know what it's like).
  • Batteries and the V-16 battery in date.
  • First-aid kit: remove expired items and restock.
  • Flashlight and power bank charged.
  • Chains checked and in their bag, not buried under everything in the trunk.

Ten minutes, twice a year. It is the cheapest maintenance there is for something that can matter to you for an entire afternoon.
