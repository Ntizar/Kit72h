Emergency folder at home: documents, cash and copies ready to evacuate (2026)

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 6 min min read

After every DANA, major fire or prolonged blackout in Spain, the same scene repeats: families who leave home with only the clothes on their backs and who, weeks later, cannot prove where they lived, what they had insured or what medication they needed. The emergency folder is the highest-return item in the entire 72-hour kit: a folder, a waterproof pouch, a USB drive and an afternoon of work that save weeks of paperwork. It does not replace the evacuation go-bag; it complements it: if the go-bag gets you through the first day, the folder gets you through the following month.

What goes inside: seven sections and nothing more

The typical mistake is stuffing in piles of “paperwork” and then not finding anything when you need to sign something. Organize it by sections, with dividers or labeled sleeves:

  • Identity: a photocopy of the DNI or NIE for each member of the household, including minors, plus the family book. The copy is enough to process paperwork even if you lose the original; if there is an evacuation, the original travels in the go-bag, not in the house.
  • Housing: the deed or a land registry extract if you own it, the rental contract if you rent, and the cadastral reference (it appears on the IBI receipt and on the Catastro electronic office). If you rent, add the owner’s or real estate agent’s contact details: without that phone number, any procedure gets stuck.
  • Insurance: home, car, and health or life insurance policies you have, with the policy number and claims phone number copied by hand on the first page. In a claim, a phone number to hand is worth more than a perfect policy.
  • Health: each person’s health card, a record of regular medication, known allergies, and the children’s vaccination records. If someone is on a chronic treatment, keep a recent prescription in the health sleeve.
  • Money and utilities: the IBAN of your accounts, mortgage or loan contract, and the latest electricity, water, gas and homeowners’ association fees bill with the customer or contract reference for each service: this is what lets you file a claim or reconnect after an outage without hunting for it in email.
  • Vehicle: registration certificate, vehicle inspection (ITV) card and insurer details. Useful if you need to travel, if the car is damaged, or if they ask for them at a checkpoint during the emergency.
  • Out-of-area contacts: two or three trusted phone numbers outside your city or region. If your area loses network coverage, someone outside can coordinate calls and messages.

The three levels: home, go-bag and cloud

A single format is not enough. Paper survives a phone with no battery and a blackout; the digital copy survives a flooded or smoke-filled house. The emergency folder works by being copied in three places at once:

Cash and cards when the power goes out

During the power outage of 28 April 2025, large areas of the country were left without card terminals and with many ATMs out of service for hours. The lesson is not “withdraw a lot of money,” but “have just enough available for two or three days, in small denominations”:

  • Discreet cash: enough for food, fuel and pharmacy for a few days, in small denominations, kept away from original documents. Do not announce it or photograph it.
  • Cards under control: write down each card number and each bank’s customer service phone number in the finance section, or keep them in your encrypted password manager. If you lose your wallet, calling the bank is the first thing to do; having the details to hand cuts the call in half.
  • Two locations for cash: some in your wallet and some in the go-bag or the car. If the emergency catches you away from home, the cash at home does not exist.
  • Sensitive files, never in the gallery: photos of documents and cards go inside a password-protected compressed file or in the phone’s encrypted notes app, not loose in the gallery.

Digital copy: do it right, once

  • Scan each document as a legible PDF, with good light and the full page: if the margin is cut off, it is no use.
  • Name files meaningfully (dni-maria.pdf, poliza-hogar.pdf), not img_4471.jpg. When you need it, you need it now.
  • Store the whole set in the cloud and on a USB drive that is not at home: work, a relative’s house or the car’s glove compartment.
  • Encrypt the package with a password that is not your phone password and write that password on paper, in a place separate from the folder. If you lose both together, the folder protects nothing.

Keep it alive: two reviews a year

An outdated folder is a false folder: it gives you the feeling of being prepared and fails just when you open it. It takes ten minutes and there are two easy dates not to forget:

  • Clock change (March and October): review policies, contracts, phone numbers, and that the minors’ documents are still valid.
  • Start of the school year: update vaccination records, medication and school or work contacts.
  • After any life change: moving house, changing bank, changing utility company or car, update the folder the same day. It is the task everyone postpones and the one you appreciate most later.

Four mistakes that cancel out the effort

  • Everything in a drawer with the clothes: if the clothes get wet, everything gets wet. A waterproof pouch costs little and solves the whole problem.
  • Originals with no copy elsewhere: a sole deed or registration certificate is no use if it is lost with the house.
  • Not digitized: arriving at a registry office or an insurer with nothing on your phone stretches any procedure by several days.
  • Never reviewed: a six-year-old folder with old phone numbers and policies is more misleading than having nothing.

Checklist for this week

  • Seven labeled sleeves or a binder with dividers.
  • Identity photocopies for everyone, including minors.
  • Housing: deed, rental contract or cadastral reference.
  • Insurance with policy number and claims phone number to hand.
  • Health: health card, medication and vaccination records.
  • Finances: IBAN, mortgage and latest bill for each utility.
  • Two contacts outside the region and cash in small denominations.
  • Everything scanned into an encrypted PDF and copied to the USB drive kept elsewhere.
