Best long-lasting canned foods for emergencies (2026)

📅 2026-10-10 · ⏱ 6 min min read

The power goes out at 21:40 and the first thing you do is open the fridge to see how much is left. After twelve hours the freezer is already warm and, after thirty-six, the question is no longer what you fancy but what can still be eaten. At that point, a well-chosen preserved food is worth more than any gadget: food that lasts months or years without refrigeration, without power and without you doing anything.

That is the official criterion: the European Union Preparedness Strategy calls for 72 hours of self-sufficiency per household and Civil Protection recommends non-perishable food and water for three days. Spain does not lack food, it lacks a plan: the Food Waste Report in Spain 2024 (MAPA) puts what we throw away each year at 1.125 billion kilos or litres, with 97.5% in households and about 24 kilos per person. We buy food that ends up in the bin while the emergency reserve does not exist.

I compare nine long-life preserved foods available in any supermarket or on Amazon Spain: how long they last unopened, what each one is really good for and its weak point.

Best-before is not use-by: the date that decides whether you throw it away or not

AESAN devotes an entire campaign to separating the two concepts, and Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 governs them. Confusing them is the main reason for throwing away a reserve that is still good:

  • Best-before date — canned and jarred foods, pulses, rice, pasta, flour, oil, honey. It indicates how long the food keeps its quality. After that date it is still safe: it may lose texture or flavour, but it is not thrown away because of the date alone.
  • Use-by date — fresh meat and fish, certain dairy products, ready meals. That one cannot be stretched: do not consume it after the date.
  • In a blackout, the fridge is the first thing you lose. Fresh food and items with a use-by date go within 24–48 hours; preserved food is the last thing to leave the plan.

Tip: write the date on each jar or tin when you store it, keep the can opener in the same drawer and apply the FIFO rule —what comes in goes at the front; what is already there gets eaten first—. That way the reserve is renewed with your normal shopping instead of expiring in a cupboard.

Winner by category

  • For the 20-year drawer: the MRE-9 ration. The only one on the list with a stated shelf life of two decades: you buy it once and do not touch it until you need it.
  • For the pantry you use every day: tuna and sardines. They perform in an emergency and they perform in March: the best reserve is the one that gets eaten before it expires.
  • To eat without heat or water: sardines with nuts. If the plan depends on lighting something, it is not a plan.
  • For space and weight: the freeze-dried ration. 140 g per meal: it fits in the evacuation backpack and does not weigh down the cupboard.
  • For the kitchen without a fridge: the jar of pulses. Already cooked, no gas or soaking needed, cold or at room temperature.

Six storage rules decided before the blackout

  1. Dry, cool and dark. Never on the garage floor or next to the boiler: moisture dents cans and heat makes the fat in fish and nuts go rancid.
  2. Out of the sun. A can left in the light all summer tastes like cardboard.
  3. Damaged packaging, out. Dented, bulging, rusty or with the lid popped, it gets thrown away without debate, even if the date is good.
  4. Once open, fridge and use up. In warm conditions, hours; never leave an open jar “for tomorrow” on the counter.
  5. Manual can opener in the same drawer. No power means no socket, and no can opener means no dinner: it is the cheapest item in the kit.
  6. Trial run. A night without power using what you already have stored teaches you more than any list: you will discover that the can opener is missing, not the tuna.

Five mistakes that leave the pantry exactly as it was on day one

  1. Buying special “emergency food”. What you do not eat in normal life you will not eat in an emergency either, and it expires sooner: build the reserve with what you already eat.
  2. Storing it in the storage room or garage. A Spanish summer in a storage room is the worst enemy of preserved food.
  3. Not checking dates when shopping. Two identical cans, two different dates: take the one that lasts longest and note it on the drawer.
  4. Cooking only if there is power. If the whole plan needs the induction hob, there is no plan in a blackout: mix ready-to-eat products with products that only need hot water.
  5. Not knowing how much you have. An inventory on the fridge door, with dates, stops you from emptying the whole cupboard “just in case”.
