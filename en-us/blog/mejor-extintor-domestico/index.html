Best home fire extinguisher 2026: 6 types and where to place it

📅 2026-10-17 · ⏱ 6 min min read

A frying pan catches fire, a power strip trips or a candle falls onto the curtain: there is very little margin between a small cooking fire and having to run out, and in Spain that margin is lost every day. In 2024, 234 people died in fires and explosions in Spain, 172 of them inside homes, and among the likely causes, electrical faults top the list, at 41.9%. These are the figures from the Fire Victim Study by Fundación MAPFRE and APTB (Professional Association of Fire Technicians), which has collected data from fire services and forensic medicine institutes since 2010.

The fire extinguisher is the cheap answer: between €25 and €60 for a fire that starts while you are at home. Regulations require them to be installed and maintained in buildings and commercial premises —RIPCI, Royal Decree 513/2017, is the reference regulation— but inside a private home there is no legal obligation. Here you decide which type puts out what, with six compared and where to hang it.

1. First decide this: what class of fire you have

A letter appears on the label of any fire extinguisher: it is the only thing that matters when buying. The UNE-EN 3-7 standard sets out the classes and the “typical household fires” that the device must put out:

ClassWhat burnsWhere you find it at home
ASolids: wood, paper, fabric, cardboardSofa, curtain, wardrobe, wastebasket
BLiquids: gasoline, solvents, oilWorkshop, garage, gas cylinders
CCombustible gasesButane, kitchen gas
DMetals (industrial use)Almost never in a home
FHot cooking fats and oilsFrying pan and deep fryer: the most common at home

The practical translation: ABC powder covers A, B and C, almost everything that burns in an apartment. What it does not cover well is a frying pan (Class F): the oil is extinguished on the surface and reignites as soon as you move it. For cooking, use a Class F extinguisher (foam or water with additive) or, better and faster, cover the pan with its lid or a fire blanket. Pouring water is the classic way to get burned: it vaporizes and carries burning oil all over the kitchen.

3. Best choice by situation

  • A single device for an apartment: 6 kg ABC powder, the same as the one hanging in stairwells; if the budget is tight, the 2 kg one already covers an incipient fire: see ABC powder extinguishers ↗.
  • Frequent cooking: Class F foam next to the door, never above the hob, plus a fire blanket in the drawer below: see Class F extinguishers ↗.
  • Electrical panel or storage room: 2 kg CO₂, which leaves the device and cables usable afterwards: see CO₂ extinguishers ↗.
  • Garage or workshop with fuel: 6 kg ABC powder on its wall bracket and visible from the entrance: see 6 kg extinguishers ↗.
  • Car trunk and caravan: fire blanket or small extinguisher, because in a van the first things to burn are the fridge and clothing — the rest goes in the car kit: see fire blankets ↗.

Honest warning: a 2 kg extinguisher empties in about 10-15 seconds of discharge. It is for a fire that has just started, not for a room in flames. If two passes have not put it out, it is time to get out.

4. Where to put it (and the one-minute monthly check)

  • Near the exit, never on the other side of the fire. Fire service rule: you do not cross the room that is burning to get it. Visible, with no furniture in front and with signage above.
  • Height and mounting. On its bracket, at hand height and away from heat, moisture and bumps. On the floor or behind a door that opens towards it: the classic mistake.
  • Monthly check (60 seconds): pressure gauge needle in the green zone, tamper seal and pin intact, hose free of cracks, body free of rust. And turn it upside down a couple of times a year so the powder does not compact.
  • Formal inspection: if it hangs in an installation subject to RIPCI, maintenance is carried out by an authorized company: annual inspection and pressure test every five years. In a private home it is not mandatory, but the same applies: service life is around 20 years from manufacture (Tecnifuego) and it must be replaced sooner if the needle does not return to green.
  • During a power outage, double-check. Candles and generators raise the risk exactly when no one is watching: check the gauge and the candle before nightfall. The details are in the power outage kit.

5. Five mistakes that make the extinguisher useless

  • Water on an electrical fire. First switch off the power if you can, then use CO₂ or powder with a dielectric test.
  • Buying by color. Red says nothing: look at the label with the A/B/C/F letters and the rating. An aerosol is not a 6 kg extinguisher.
  • Covering it with the washing machine or boxes. The one that has been in the storage room for years does not exist.
  • Overconfidence. The extinguisher is for a pan fire. If the flame reaches the ceiling or there is dense smoke, close the door and get out.
  • Forgetting the rest of the system. The extinguisher does not warn you: that is what the smoke detector is for, and the flashlight helps you avoid lighting unnecessary fires. All of that is in the fire kit and in passive fire safety and electrical fires.

6. If it breaks out: four steps, in this order

  1. Raise the alarm: call 112 before touching the extinguisher, with the address and what is burning. Wrong information is worse than late information.
  2. Protect the exit: extinguisher in hand, upright, hose aimed at the base of the fire and one step from the door.
  3. One attempt only: two short passes. If it does not go down, drop the extinguisher, close the door behind you and get out without grabbing anything else.
  4. Never go back until the fire service authorizes it, even if “only ashes remain”: smoke is what kills.
