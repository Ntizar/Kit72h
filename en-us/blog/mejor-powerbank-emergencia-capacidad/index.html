Best power bank for emergency kit 2026: 4 models and real mAh

📅 2026-10-03 · ⏱ 5 min min read

The blackout of 28 April 2025 left half the country without power and phones at 2%. Most people solved it with the power bank they had charged in a drawer. But if the power goes out for several days, that drawer runs out: in 24-48 hours your phone becomes a glass weight, and with it streaming radio, maps, family WhatsApp and the phone flashlight. This is the guide to choosing the emergency power bank that actually keeps you going for 72 hours — with the real capacity you need, the honest figure brands hide (mAh) and four models compared.

How much capacity you really need for 72 hours

A modern smartphone has a 4,000-5,000 mAh battery. On a normal day, one full charge lasts; in an emergency you will use the phone much more (calls, WhatsApp, flashlight, maps), so the realistic estimate is 2 charges per day. On that basis:

  • 72 hours, 1 phone: 10,000-20,000 mAh.
  • 72 hours, 2 phones (couple or family): 20,000-30,000 mAh.
  • 7 days or more: 30,000 mAh + portable solar panel to recharge during the day.

There is a nuance no product listing tells you and that changes the purchase: the advertised mAh are not what reaches your phone. A 20,000 mAh battery at 3.7 V stores about 74 Wh; when converting to USB output (5-9 V) and with electronics losses, what actually arrives is between 13,000 and 14,500 mAh — that is, 65% to 72% efficiency, the range independent labs measure with known-brand units. In plain terms: a 20,000 mAh power bank gives 4 full phone charges, not the 8-10 the brochure suggests. With 10,000 real mAh (about 2 charges) you just get through 72 hours; with 4 charges, comfortably. That is why 20,000 mAh is the recommended minimum and not a whim.

What to require from an emergency power bank (the checklist)

  1. Capacity: 20,000 mAh minimum — 4 full phone charges, the number that gets you through 72 hours without anxiety.
  2. Dual output — charge two people’s phones at once, or phone + radio, without taking turns.
  3. USB-C input and output — recharging the power bank itself takes 3-4 hours and it is the connector you already have at home. If it also supports Power Delivery (PD), it can charge tablets and some light laptops too.
  4. Real percentage indicator — the usual four LEDs lie halfway through the charge; a numeric display (or percentage) tells you how much is really left when you decide whether to turn on the light or not.
  5. Built-in LED flashlight — it costs no extra and in the kit it serves as a backup if your main flashlight runs out.
  6. Shock-resistant — it falls off the table at 3 a.m. in the dark; it has to keep working.

Typical mistakes with the kit’s power bank

  • Buying it discharged. The power bank is checked like the rest of the kit: 100% charged and rotated every 3-6 months. A battery that has been in the drawer at 10% for a year will not save you the night you need it.
  • Trusting the mAh on the label. 20,000 real mAh are 4 phone charges; if the price is suspiciously low, distrust the real capacity (with unknown brands it is common to deliver half).
  • Using it as a permanent tap. Full discharge and recharge extend cell life; keeping it plugged into the wall at 100% all year does it no favors.
  • Forgetting the cable. A power bank without a cable is an elegant brick. Store it with the battery, not in another drawer, and keep a spare if you depend on USB-C.
  • Overlooking flights. If you are going to evacuate by car it does not apply, but if you have to fly: IATA (and European EASA) allows up to 100 Wh without authorization (≈27,000 mAh), always in carry-on baggage and never in the hold; between 100 and 160 Wh you need airline permission, and above 160 Wh you cannot fly. A 20,000 mAh (74 Wh) travels without problems.

And if the blackout lasts more than three days

The power bank is the reserve, not the power plant: it runs out, full stop. To go beyond 72 hours, the combination that works is power bank + 30-50 W portable solar panel. In summer sun, a 30-50 W panel recharges a 20,000 mAh every 2-3 days (in winter, multiply by two or three); that way the phone and radio do not depend on the power coming back. You have the real watt calculations in portable solar panel for emergencies and the full combo in 72-hour solar generator. And do not forget the radio: official information comes via hand-crank radio, which does not use up your battery reserve.

The verdict

A 20,000 mAh power bank with USB-C and dual output is the 72h kit standard: 4 phone charges, 74 Wh (flies without paperwork) and 25-50 € well spent. If you have a partner or children, add a second 10,000-20,000 mAh one — the cheap redundancy that turns a scare into a minor detail. And remember the only rule that matters: it comes out of the drawer 100% charged and is rotated every 3-6 months. The full kit list, with the energy already sorted, is in the basic 72-hour kit.
