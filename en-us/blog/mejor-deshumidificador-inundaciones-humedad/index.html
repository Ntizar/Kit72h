Best dehumidifier for floods and damp: 4 types compared

📅 2026-10-25 · ⏱ 5 min min read

Hidden humidity is what destroys the most after a flood or a rainy season. You do not need water to enter the house for walls, clothes, or particleboard furniture to be ruined: 70% relative humidity is enough for the first molds to appear, and above 75% the Stachybotrys fungus—the toxin-producing one—starts to proliferate. In Spain, INMET is clear: ideal indoor relative humidity ranges between 40% and 60%. Above 65%, problems start.

The dehumidifier is the tool that controls that humidity when there is not enough heating to dry the walls or when the rain has left a layer of water that cannot be dried with a cloth. And in a post-flood blackout scenario, when the grid fails and you cannot turn on the car fan to ventilate the living room, it is the difference between recovering your things and burying them.

I compare the three types available in Spain—compressor, desiccant, and membrane—by their power consumption, real capacity, and usefulness in an emergency.

Approximate prices in Spain: 20 L/day compressor between 150-300 €, desiccant between 100-200 €, and Peltier between 30-60 €. Compressor units perform better but use more power: if the blackout lasts, a 300 W compressor on a small generator can empty the fuel tank before the heater does.

Best in each category

Highest capacity: the 30-50 L/day compressor. Ideal for a flooded basement or an apartment after a DANA. Brands such as De'Longhi, Pro Breeze, or Mila offer 20-30 L/day models at a reasonable price. View compressor dehumidifiers on Amazon.

Best for cold: the salt-based desiccant. At 5 °C it still extracts 10 L/day, while the compressor drops to 3-4 L. If you live in mountain areas or areas with harsh winters, it is the most reliable option when there is no heating. View desiccant dehumidifiers on Amazon.

Best size/performance ratio: the 3-5 L Peltier for cabinets or small rooms. It is not suitable for a flooded living room, but it is enough to prevent the cabinet from filling with mold after a period of heavy rain. View small dehumidifiers on Amazon.

Free: cross-ventilation with borax (borax crystals, not pure borax). A small borax bag in each cabinet passively absorbs 1-2 L/day. It is not a dehumidifier, but during a blackout it is the option that needs no plug. Borax is sold in hardware stores for 2-5 € per kilo and does not expire.

When to buy it and how to use it

There are two times to use a dehumidifier in an emergency:

  1. Prevention (before): if you live in an area with frequent rain or in a ground-floor apartment, having a 10-15 L/day compressor in the storage room or under the kitchen is like having a closed umbrella that does not get in the way. Plug it in during the rainy season and keep humidity between 45-55%. Power consumption is low: a 15 L compressor uses about 250 Wh/day ≈ 0,04 €/day.
  2. Recovery (after a flood): after a DANA or a flash flood, wet walls take weeks to dry on their own. A 20-30 L compressor will dry a 70 m² home in 3-5 days if it is kept closed (windows closed, room door sealed with plastic). Without one, it can take 2-3 weeks, and mold can appear in 48-72 h.

Three-day rule: if after a flood you cannot dry the home within 72 hours, you will need a medium- or high-capacity dehumidifier (20+ L/day). Engineered wood materials (MDF, particleboard) that get wet and are not dried in time swell and permanently lose their structure: an MDF table that gets wet never returns to the same condition even if it dries.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the windows open: a dehumidifier does not work if the outside is more humid. During a storm or right after, outdoor air can be 95% humidity; opening the window is like adding fuel to the fire. Close everything and let the machine work.
  • Buying a Peltier for a living room: the 3 L Peltier only works for a drawer or a small cabinet. If the area to dry exceeds 30 m², get a compressor of at least 15 L/day.
  • Not emptying the tank: a 20 L compressor in a very humid apartment can fill up in 6-8 hours. If it fills and you do not empty it, the machine stops and humidity rises suddenly. Set up continuous drainage or remember to empty it every 4-6 hours.
  • Believing that it ventilates: a dehumidifier does not renew the air: it only dries it. If there is visible mold, you need cleaning with diluted bleach (1 part bleach to 10 parts water) before drying. The dehumidifier prevents it from reappearing, but it does not eliminate what is already growing.

Humidity is the number one reason insurers reject post-flood claims: if mold has been growing for more than 30 days, it is considered “progressive deterioration” and not “sudden damage.” That is why acting in the first 72 hours is not a suggestion; it is a contractual obligation.
