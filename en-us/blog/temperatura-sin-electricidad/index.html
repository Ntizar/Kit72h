Cold and heat without electricity: safe home temperature (2026)

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 2 min min read

In a January blackout, the enemy isn't the darkness: it's the accumulated cold. In a heat wave without air conditioning, it's dehydration. Both problems can be solved with cheap materials and a little physics.

Keeping warm

  • Choose one room and close it off: less volume to heat (or to keep warm). Small interior rooms hold heat better.
  • Insulate from below: the floor steals more heat than you think. Rugs, blankets or cardboard underfoot.
  • Layering works better than one thick garment: the air trapped between layers is the real insulation.
  • The emergency thermal blanket (the gold ones for 2 €) reflects body heat; it works better inside a normal blanket, not on its own.
  • A tent indoors: it sounds absurd, but a small tent in the living room creates a microclimate that raises the temperature by several degrees. Mountaineers have always done it.
  • If you use a butane heater or burning charcoal, ventilate: carbon monoxide kills without any smell. Never in a closed bedroom.

Cooling down

  • Cross-ventilation at dawn and at night; windows closed and blinds down during the day. The golden rule: open when it's cooler outside than inside, close when it isn't.
  • Add moisture and move air: a damp sheet in front of the window or a spray bottle with any breeze lowers the perceived temperature by several degrees.
  • Cool the key points: wrists, nape of the neck, neck and ankles. Cold water on those pulse points cools the blood faster than soaking your whole body.
  • Drink before you feel thirsty and avoid alcohol and heavy meals; the body heats up as it digests.

Warning signs

Uncontrollable shivering, confusion or drowsiness (hypothermia); hot, dry skin with no sweating, severe headache or vomiting (heatstroke): these are medical emergencies. Call 112 as soon as possible.
