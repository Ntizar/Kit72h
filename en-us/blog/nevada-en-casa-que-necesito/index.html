Snowed in at home: 8 key tips to get through days without power (2026)

📅 2026-10-13 · ⏱ 6 min min read

When AEMET's warnings map turns orange, the problem is not picture-postcard snow: it is the days that follow. Roads cut off, power that does not come back, heating down and the supermarket with empty shelves. Storm Filomena (January 2021) is still the benchmark in Spain: AEMET itself describes it as an exceptional event, with snow accumulation of up to 50 cm in Madrid city centre and minimum temperatures of -26.5 °C. In many homes what failed was not that it snowed: it was that no one had prepared for three days without shopping.

This is the list of what I need at home when a snow warning is issued, in order and with the reason why. If you only do one thing, do the first.

1. Check the official warning, not the WhatsApp group

It all starts with the AEMET warnings map: yellow (be careful), orange (significant risk) and red (extreme risk, with implications for your safety). Supplement it with Protección Civil recommendations and with 112 if there is a real emergency.

And before moving the car, DGT repeats it in every winter warning: check the weather forecast and road conditions before setting out, and have chains or winter tyres to hand. Traffic authorities may restrict traffic for vehicles without that equipment and may even close roads preventively. The practical rule: if the journey is not essential, don't make it.

2. Heating: what warms you and what kills

When the power goes out, what kills in winter is not the cold but carbon monoxide: odourless, invisible and produced by any poorly ventilated combustion. It is the first rule on the list:

  • Never run a generator inside the home, not in the garage, not in the entrance hall, not right next to a window. Why, in the generator vs power station comparison: in a flat, a power station is the only option.
  • Gas or combustion heater: only with the room ventilated and never while you sleep. A butane gas heater handles a living room, not a house closed up all night.
  • Battery-powered carbon monoxide detector on every floor and near the bedrooms. It costs less than a dinner out and it is the only thing that warns you before it is too late: battery-powered CO detector.
  • Keep the house warm, not just the people: cover the window at night with a curtain or blanket, close off rooms you are not using and wear layers at home. The detail is in maintaining body temperature without electricity and in dressing in layers.

The cold-specific items are in the cold and snow kit.

3. Water and food: three days without being able to shop

A snowstorm cuts roads and delays deliveries, so plan for at least 3 litres of water per person per day (drinking, hygiene and cooking): the figure and how to store it are in how much water to store per person. Buy food you can eat without gas and without cooking: canned goods, tinned pulses, nuts, sliced bread, coffee and a can opener (if you forget the manual one, a knife and a pot are not enough).

And as soon as the power goes out, the order is: use fresh food first (meat, fish, dairy), then frozen food. The fridge lasts hours, not days; the full timetable is in storing food without a fridge, and if you live alone or want extra margin, in portable cooler for emergencies.

5. The car: chains, tyres and common sense

DGT repeats it every winter: carry chains or winter or all-season tyres if you are going to drive in areas with a snow warning, check road conditions before setting out and keep the left lane clear for snowploughs. If the snow catches you on the road: do not abandon the vehicle, slow down and call 112.

The gear goes in the boot before the storm is announced: snow chains, a pair of waterproof gloves (fitting chains with frozen hands is impossible) and a thermal blanket. The full vehicle list is in the car kit.

6. Neighbours, older people and those who live alone

In a building, logistics are worked out among everyone: who shops if someone cannot go out and who has the key to the older person's flat. A call at the same time every day to someone who lives alone is not courtesy, it is a welfare check: the script is in living alone and being prepared, the equipment in the older person's kit and the phone numbers you cannot do without in official emergency resources. If there is daily medication, check the week's dose and keep the prescription up to date: chronic medication without a pharmacy.

7. The five mistakes repeated in every snowstorm

  • Going out "to have a look" while snow is falling. The road that "is fine" at ten may be closed at twelve.
  • Running the generator or heater in an enclosed space. It is the mistake that causes the most hospitalisations in winter.
  • Buying only food that has to be cooked and discovering at 21:00 that there is no gas, water or can opener.
  • Keeping phone numbers and documents only on the mobile. With no power and the battery at 8%, the contact list does not exist: print it. The full folder is in emergency folder and documents.
  • Waiting for the neighbour to warn you. When the supermarket is overwhelmed, it is already too late to buy what matters: batteries, spare batteries, first-aid kit and medication.
