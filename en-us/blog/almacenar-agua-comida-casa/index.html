How to store water and food at home so it doesn't spoil (2026)

📅 2026-08-31 · ⏱ 2 min min read

Buying the kit is the easy part. Storing it properly so it still works the day you need it (sometimes years later) is the part almost everyone gets wrong. Rules that apply just as much to a bag of rice as to a radio:

The three enemies: heat, light and moisture

The ideal place is cool, dark and dry: an interior hallway cupboard is usually better than the typical storage room next to the boiler or on the terrace. Never directly on the floor (moisture, pests) or next to cleaning products (fumes contaminate even sealed containers).

Water: the calendar rules

  • Sealed commercial containers: replace every 6-12 months. It is not that the water expires: the container degrades and residual chlorine evaporates.
  • Store it away from sunlight and strong odors; plastic absorbs petrol, bleach or detergents.
  • If there is advance warning (storm, water outage), fill the bathtub, buckets and pots: this is non-drinking water, perfect for the toilet tank and hygiene.
  • To store your own water long term, use only new food-grade containers with airtight lids.

Food: first in, first out

  • Write the purchase date on each pack with a marker. Without a date, in six months you will no longer know what is old.
  • Real rotation: use the emergency pantry in your day-to-day cooking and restock when you shop. A reserve that is never eaten gets thrown away; one that is rotated never expires.
  • Approximate shelf life when stored well: cans and jars 3-5 years (or more), rice and pasta 2-3 years, dehydrated/military food 2-5 years, powdered milk 2-3 years sealed. Bars and nuts: much less, about a year.
  • Discard bulging cans, cans rusted at the seams or with deep dents: botulism is non-negotiable.

Inventory every 6 months

The same reminder as for water: check expiry dates, container condition, radio and flashlight batteries, and that the document folder is still complete. Half an hour twice a year keeps your kit alive; without it, in two years you have a storage room full of expired stuff.
