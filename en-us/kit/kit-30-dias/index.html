The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Buying 30 days of food and not being able to cook it: without gas or electricity, raw rice is decoration. Secure a cooking method and fuel first.
  2. Not calculating water: 3 L per person per day is 180 L for two people for a month. It's what runs out first and takes up the most space.
  3. Not rotating. A pantry you don't touch is garbage in three years; one you eat from day to day never expires.
  4. Not accounting for the bathroom: without water, 96 rolls are not enough and hygiene becomes the household's sanitation problem.
  5. Doing it all at once and at the worst price. Build it over 4–6 months by adding two packs per shopping trip, and it costs half as much.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Calculate actual consumption: 2 people, 30 days = 60 L of drinking water + 40–60 L for basic hygiene
  • Prioritize food you already eat: an emergency pantry you don't rotate is money thrown away
  • Store everything in a cool, dark, dry place; write purchase dates on each pack
  • Add your household-specific items to your kit: regular medication, diapers, pet food

→ During

  • Start consuming by expiry date: fridge first, then freezer (if kept closed, it holds for 24–48h), then pantry
  • Ration water per person, not per day: 3 L/day per adult for drinking is the minimum
  • Cook with the stove in a ventilated area, never inside a closed room

→ After

  • Restock what you consume the following week, marking the dates again
  • Check that cans aren't bulging or rusty and replace them
  • Note what was left over and what was missing: adjust quantities for the next review
