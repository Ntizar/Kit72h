Who it's for. For anyone who goes hiking, canyoning or mountain skiing. 90% of mountain rescues in Spain involve people who lost their way, got injured or were caught out by nightfall. You don't need to be an expert: going a little further than planned is enough.

When it isn't enough. This is not high-mountain gear: for glaciers, via ferratas or overnight stays at altitude you need technical equipment and training. The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Going out in city clothes and trusting the weather app. In the mountains the temperature drops by 10 degrees as soon as you climb above a ravine, and fog can arrive in 20 minutes.
  2. A single flashlight for the group, with no spare batteries, at the bottom of the backpack.
  3. Not carrying an extra layer 'because I'm doing the route in two hours': half an hour waiting for rescue in a gully happens at zero degrees.
  4. Drinking little and arriving at the end dehydrated and with heatstroke, especially in spring with sun and a backpack.
  5. Relying on your phone for navigation. It runs out, gets dropped, gets wet. Map and compass, and know how to use them before you need them.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Always share your route and return time with someone
  • Check the weather warning (AEMET) before setting out
  • Download the 112 app for your region
  • Never wear new shoes or socks on a long hike: 74% of pilgrims on the Camino de Santiago get blisters in the first few days (Colexio de Podólogos de Galicia)

→ During

  • If you get lost, stay put and make yourself visible
  • Ration your water and don't stray from the known route
  • Use trekking poles if you can: they cut impact on knees and ankles by up to 30%
  • If you feel rubbing: stop and apply the hydrocolloid dressing before a blister forms (Compeed, Scholl or similar)

→ After

  • Replenish and check the kit after every outing
  • Note down what you were missing for next time
  • Dry and store the raincoat: moisture builds mold and it loses its waterproofing
