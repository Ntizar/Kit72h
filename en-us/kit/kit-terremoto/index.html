Who it's for. For people living in a seismic zone (mainland southeast: Murcia, Almería, Granada, Málaga, Alicante) and, in fact, for any home: most damage in an earthquake is not from the quake itself, it is from falling objects and gas and power cuts. The kit is the same one that serves you for a power outage.

When it isn't enough. It is not a rescue or rubble kit: it doesn't turn you into a firefighter. Nor does it replace structural reinforcement or knowing whether your building is one that holds up: that can't be solved with a backpack.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Running to the stairs as soon as the shaking starts. Most injuries occur while fleeing: in a stairwell, with people and with falling objects. The right place is under a sturdy table.
  2. Sleeping without shoes nearby and without a flashlight at hand. A nighttime earthquake + glass-covered floor = cut feet and darkness, just when you need to get out and help.
  3. Lighting a match, turning on the light or using your mobile phone when you smell gas. It is the spark that causes the explosion. Shut off the gas first, then everything else.
  4. Not knowing where the gas shut-off valve is or not having a tool to close it. It is the first thing to do and almost no one has ever touched it.
  5. Confusing “the house is still standing” with “the house is safe”. You need to check for new cracks, doors that will not close and damp before sleeping inside: aftershocks bring down what the first tremor left weakened.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Anchor shelves, the TV and tall wardrobes to the wall: the No. 1 cause of minor injuries in an earthquake is things falling from walls
  • Locate the gas and water shut-off valve and check that it turns: after the earthquake you'll have 30 seconds and shaking hands
  • Leave closed-toe shoes and a flashlight next to the bed (under the bed, tied together): at night, broken glass on the floor is the biggest cause of cuts
  • Agree on a meeting point outside the building and a contact outside the province: local lines get overloaded, outside ones don't
  • Keep the backpack near the exit door, not in the basement storage room

→ During

  • Drop, cover under a sturdy table and hold on to the furniture: the furniture moves with you and protects you from falling objects
  • Don't run out to the stairs or use the elevator: most injuries happen while fleeing and in stairwells
  • Move away from windows, mirrors, shelves and façades. If you are outside, go to an open space away from cornices and cables
  • If you are in a car, stop in an open area, not under a bridge, streetlights or power lines
  • If you smell gas, do not turn anything on: no lights, no matches, and no mobile phone near a leak

→ After

  • Put on closed-toe shoes before walking around the house: broken glass is the leading cause of injuries and you cannot see it in the dark
  • Shut off the gas and water if there is a smell or damage, and do not turn on switches if you suspect a leak
  • Wait and listen to the radio: aftershocks are the norm in the following hours, and instructions come via radio stations and ES-Alert
  • Do not enter damaged buildings or use the elevator; check the structure before staying (new cracks, doors that will not close)
  • Record damage with photos and report it to your insurer and the local council: aid is assigned by declared areas, and that requires documentation
