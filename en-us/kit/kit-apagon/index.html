Who it's for. For everyone. On 28 April 2025 the peninsula went 12 hours or more without power: traffic lights out, ATMs closed, lifts stopped, supermarkets without card payment terminals. A long blackout is not a minor anecdote: it is a day without being able to buy, without water on upper floors and without a lift.

When it isn't enough. It won't help if what you expect is to keep working or studying as normal: that requires the ability to generate electricity (a large panel, a home battery), which is another level of investment. And if you live on a fifth floor without a lift, bear in mind that the water you go down to fetch has to be carried back up on foot.

04 / Safety during the outage

A blackout changes how you cook and how you light your home, and that brings two invisible risks: carbon monoxide and fire. They are two small purchases that are almost never used and that, on the day they are needed, are the only ones that matter.

  • Battery-powered carbon monoxide (CO) detector with alarm CO has no smell, is invisible and does not irritate: it causes drowsiness and headaches and, if you stay in the room, it kills you in your sleep. During a blackout people light candles, burners and braziers, and that is where it appears. Put it at head height, outside the bathroom and near the cooking area, and press the test button every time the clocks change. Non-negotiable rule: braziers, barbecues, generators and burners never in a closed room or in the garage. ~ $18-$32

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Opening the fridge every so often to see what's inside. Kept closed, a fridge holds for about 4 hours and a full freezer for up to 24-48 hours.
  2. Lighting candles and leaving them unattended: the classic cause of home fires during blackouts. With kids, a flashlight, period.
  3. Not unplugging appliances: when power returns, the surge can fry the fridge, the boiler or the router.
  4. Relying on your phone having battery: during blackouts everyone calls at once and the antennas don't hold up long. Airplane mode and a full power bank.
  5. Paying by card and discovering the card terminal doesn't work. Cash in small bills is the plan, not the detail.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Charge phones and power banks daily if there is a warning of grid strain
  • Keep cash at home: card payment terminals don't work without power
  • A gas burner or camping stove to eat something hot
  • Put two frozen bottles of water or gel blocks inside the freezer: on the day of the outage they go into the fridge and give you hours of cold without opening the door.
  • Test your CO detector today with its test button and check that the fire extinguisher gauge is in the green. In a blackout there is no time to discover they don't work.

→ During

  • Don't use candles as your main light source: always use a flashlight
  • Unplug sensitive appliances to avoid surges when the power comes back
  • Save phone battery: calls and SMS only (the data network may go down)
  • No combustion appliances in a closed room: no braziers, barbecues, generators or gas stoves inside the home or garage. If you light the stove, cross-ventilate (one window and another opening) and keep the CO detector in sight.
  • Sort the fridge by thawing order: milk, meat, fish and cold cuts first; frozen and canned goods last. Anything already thawed gets cooked or thrown out: don't refreeze.
  • With a UPS, turn off the computer and leave only the router: you keep wifi while the battery lasts and avoid the voltage spike when power comes back.

→ After

  • Recharge everything again and restock what you used
  • Check food that has lost the cold chain
  • Before going to sleep, air out the room where you used a flame and check the CO detector and fire extinguisher again.
  • Restock what you used: batteries, candles, freezer gel and anything you opened from the pantry. The next outage won't warn you.
