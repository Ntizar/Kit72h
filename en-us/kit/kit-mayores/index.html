Who it's for. Households with people aged 70 or over, especially if they live alone, take chronic medication or rely on hearing aids, glasses or assisted mobility. A family member puts it together in an afternoon and it is checked against the prescription folder.

When it isn't enough. It does not replace telecare or the carers' plan: it is equipment, not a service. And if the person needs daily health care (oxygen, dialysis), the plan is discussed with their health centre, not with this list.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Not having 2 weeks of reserve chronic medication. That is THE mistake: after a disaster, the pharmacy opens late or doesn't open, and stopping anticoagulants, antihypertensives or insulin is an emergency.
  2. Forgetting hearing aid batteries: without hearing, the person doesn't hear the radio, doesn't understand the warning and doesn't ask for help. A blister pack of spare batteries in every kit.
  3. Trusting the mobile phone: small screens, small print and no one to help them dial. A landline without electricity still works (analog ones do), and paper contacts don't run out.
  4. Not preparing for a fall: without power, a carpeted hallway with no night light is a broken hip. LED night lights cost 10 € and prevent 50% of the scenario.
  5. Store everything in the high kitchen cupboard. If you evacuate, the oldest person's 24-hour bag must be within easy reach, with their name and contact details.
  6. Do not overlook incontinence. Without a washing machine or hot water, a single day without enough spare supplies forces a choice between hygiene and drinking water. It can be sorted with 15-30 € in the cupboard long before it is needed.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Make the medical ID card with the person: illnesses, medication with doses, allergies, doctor, contacts. It is updated every 6 months at the review.
  • Ask the doctor or nurse for a 2-week plan of chronic medication: in Spain, the electronic prescription allows most treatments to be collected in advance; your doctor will tell you how.
  • Practise using the card and the telecare phone number with the person, not just in front of them: in an emergency, 'they already know' is not enough.
  • Agree with neighbours and family who calls whom and what warning signal you use (call, message, knocking on the wall: during power cuts some people use a whistle).
  • Count how many times they get up at night to go to the toilet: if it is more than once, the urinal with a handle, a night light in the hallway and a non-slip mat come before any other product on the list.
  • Keep the medication in a bag with a handle that the person can hang over their shoulder, not in stacked boxes: when leaving quickly, people only grab what they can pick up with one hand.

→ During

  • Medication first: if you have to decide, chronic treatment is not skipped; the kit's reserve is for that.
  • With hearing aids without batteries or broken glasses, the person becomes isolated and disoriented: check that they can hear and see before anything else.
  • Heat or cold: older people regulate temperature worse and don't notice thirst. In a heatwave with no power, keep water in sight and set a drinking schedule, even if they don't ask.
  • Don't let them make decisions alone if they are confused: heatstroke and dehydration cause confusion before weakness sets in.
  • Without a washing machine or hot water, incontinence is the day-2 problem: use incontinence pads and a separate bag for dirty clothes. Don't hand-wash with your reserve water: that water is for drinking.
  • If the phone fails and she lives alone, a whistle and rhythmic knocks on the wall are how a neighbor can locate her. Agree this beforehand with the neighbor and with her, using a specific signal (three knocks, pause, three knocks).

→ After

  • Check used-up medication and restock the kit the same day with the prescription.
  • Check hearing aid batteries, pacemaker if applicable, and flashlights: after a scare, many things get used without being replaced.
  • Talk to the health center if treatment was interrupted: sometimes it has to be prescribed again.
  • Encourage them to talk about what they went through: after a long emergency, an older person's emotional crash is as real as a burst pipe.
  • Check the skin in folds and pressure areas if they have been unable to wash for more than 24 hours: any irritation or red area that doesn't recover should be discussed with their nurse or doctor, not treated at home.
  • Write down which product was actually missing and restock it that same day: the kit is corrected by what was used, not by what you thought you would use.
