The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Relying on the mobile alert and having nothing ready until it goes off. In the 2024 DANA many people had no time to spare: the kit has to be ready beforehand.
  2. Going down to the garage or the storage room to save things once it's already raining hard. It's the classic accident.
  3. Turning on the light or touching wet sockets: electrocution on the ground floor is one of the most common causes of death in floods.
  4. Going back too early and walking through the mud without gloves or boots: it's full of sewage, glass and metal.
  5. Not noting down or photographing anything: damage claims go wrong without evidence and the deadlines of the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros keep running. Take dated photos and video from day one, including of what you throw out. And don't store the kit in the basement: the first place to flood is the worst spot for emergency supplies.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Keep the basic 72h kit within reach
  • Do not leave loose objects on terraces or in gardens that could be swept away
  • If you live in a flood-prone area, prepare sandbags and get out of the basement
  • With an orange or red warning, keep alerts enabled on your phone: the new Basic Flood Guideline (BOE, 27/04/2026) consolidates ES-Alert as a direct warning channel for the public
  • Check the warnings before it rains, not when it is already coming down: on AEMET (aemet.es, Warnings section) or Meteoalarm, the European warning service. Civil Protection considers torrential rain to be above 60 mm/hour; with an orange or red warning, the plan for the day changes: don't go out to run errands.
  • Move the kit, documents and charger today to a cupboard on the highest floor. On the ground floor, in the basement or garage, they get soaked just when you need them and the water won't let you go down. It's free and takes five minutes.

→ During

  • Do not drive or walk through flooded areas: 15 cm of water can sweep a person away
  • Go up to upper floors if water enters the house; do not use the elevator
  • Switch off the electricity if there is a risk of contact with water
  • If water reaches you in the car, leave it and go up to a tall building: a car floats and is swept away by less than half a meter of moving water. Never cross an underpass, a ford or a dry riverbed, either by car or on foot: 15 cm of water can already sweep a person away.
  • Don't go down to the garage to move the car and don't go out to watch the floodwater: that's where serious DANA accidents happen. Stay on an upper floor with the radio on, and call or message anyone who lives alone nearby.

→ After

  • Don't drink tap water until you've confirmed it's safe to drink
  • Don't turn on lights or devices until you've checked that the wiring is dry
  • Photograph the damage for your insurance before you move anything
  • When cleaning up mud, wear an FFP2 mask, gloves and boots: dry mud gets inhaled and it comes from sewage. Open everything wide and dry out in the first 48 hours, or mold will stay in the walls and furniture.
  • Before drinking or cooking with tap water, check with your local council that the supply is safe: after a flood there may be a do-not-drink notice. If in the slightest doubt, bottled water or boil it for one minute.
