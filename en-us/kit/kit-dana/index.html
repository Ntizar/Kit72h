The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Relying on the mobile alert and having nothing ready until it sounds. In the 2024 DANA, many people had no time: the kit must be prepared beforehand.
  2. Going down to the garage or storage room to save things when it's already raining hard. That's the typical accident.
  3. Turning on the light or touching wet sockets: electrocution on the ground floor is one of the most common causes of death in floods.
  4. Returning too early and walking through mud without gloves or boots: it's full of sewage, glass and metal.
  5. Not noting or photographing anything: damage claims are poorly declared without evidence and the deadlines of the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros run. Take dated photos and video from the first day, including of what you throw away. And don't store the kit in the basement: the place that floods first is the worst for emergency supplies.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Keep the basic 72h kit within reach
  • Don't leave loose objects on terraces or in gardens that could be swept away
  • If you live in a flood-prone area, prepare sandbags and get out of the basement
  • With an orange or red warning, keep alerts enabled on your phone: the new Basic Directive on flooding (BOE, 27/04/2026) consolidates ES-Alert as a direct channel for warning the public
  • Check the warnings before it rains, not once it's already coming down: at AEMET (aemet.es, Warnings section) or Meteoalarm, the European warning service. Civil Protection classifies torrential rain as above 60 mm/hour; with an orange or red warning, the day's plan changes: don't go out to run errands.
  • Move the kit, the documents and the charger up to a cupboard on the highest floor today. On the ground floor, in the basement or the garage they get soaked right when you need them, and the water won't let you go down. It's free and takes five minutes.

→ During

  • Don't drive or walk through flooded areas: 15 cm of water can sweep a person away
  • Go up to the higher floors if water enters the house; don't use the elevator
  • Turn off the electricity if there is a risk of contact with water
  • If water reaches you in the car, leave it and go up to a tall building: a car floats and is swept away with less than half a metre of moving water. Never cross an underpass, a ford or a dry riverbed, whether by car or on foot: 15 cm of water is enough to knock a person off their feet.
  • Don't go down to the garage to move the car and don't go out to watch the flood: that's where serious accidents happen during a DANA. Stay on an upper floor with the radio on, and call or message anyone living alone nearby.

→ After

  • Don't drink tap water until you've confirmed it's safe to drink
  • Don't turn on lights or devices until you've checked that the electrical installation is dry
  • Photograph the damage for insurance before removing anything
  • When cleaning up mud, wear an FFP2 mask, gloves and boots: dried mud is inhaled and comes from sewage. Open all windows and dry within the first 48 hours, or mold will stay in the walls and furniture.
  • Before drinking or cooking with tap water, check with your local council that the supply is safe: after a flood there may be a boil-water notice. If in any doubt, use bottled water or boil it for one minute.
