Who it's for. For homes with gas or electric heating (both fail in a blackout), for anyone living in a snow or frost area, and for older people and babies, who lose body temperature before anyone else. Filomena left half of Spain without heating and unable to leave the house.

When it isn't enough. It is not a mountaineering or extreme mountain snow kit: that requires technical gear and training. Nor does it replace the basic rule: if you can't keep the house above 15 degrees, the plan is to go somewhere warm.

Approximate cost. €80-180 (half that if you use the winter clothing you already have)

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Using the stove or heater in a closed room. It is the leading cause of CO poisoning in blackouts and snowstorms: the appliance consumes oxygen and gives off carbon monoxide.
  2. Trying to heat the whole house. Without heating you don't heat a house, you heat one room: choosing a small one and closing off the rest is the difference between 12 and 18 degrees.
  3. Dressing the baby or the older person "with one more layer" of cotton. Over cotton you need a layer that cuts the wind and stays dry; and older people must be moved out of the house when their hands turn cold and pale.
  4. Storing the power bank and the phone in the car. Cold kills their battery: at 0 degrees they lose up to half their charge and don't recover well.
  5. Going out into the snow without a reflective item or a scraper. Most deaths in snowstorms aren't from cold, they're from traffic accidents and pedestrians being hit.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Check you have a carbon monoxide detector before using any combustion appliance indoors (heaters, burners, generators): CO has no smell and no colour and kills in closed rooms
  • Prepare a «refuge» room: the smallest one with the fewest windows, to heat it and have everyone live there instead of trying to heat the whole house
  • Store the draught excluders/window sealing film before it freezes: sticking them on at -4 degrees won't hold
  • Put the winter kit in the car before the snowfall, not during it (check that the chains are your size and the tyres aren't low)
  • Check on older neighbours: cold indoors isn't noticed, it builds up

→ During

  • Never use a combustion heater or stove in a room without ventilation: leave a gap open and turn the appliance off before sleeping
  • Dress in layers (thermal, liner, windproof) and cover your head, hands and feet: that's where more than half the heat is lost
  • Drink water even if you're not thirsty: in the cold you drink less and dehydration makes hypothermia worse
  • Don't go out to clear snow on an empty stomach or in wet shoes; and don't take the car without a scraper and full visibility
  • If you have to sleep at low temperature, do it in pairs and fully dressed: share the sleeping bag or blanket

→ After

  • Check and clean the CO detector and restock batteries and fuel the same day: the second wave usually arrives within a week
  • Suspect frozen pipes: open the taps slowly before turning on the boiler; a pressure surge will burst the system
  • Restock salt, fuel and spent batteries: in snow, whatever runs out runs out across the whole region at once
  • Check the state of the car (battery, antifreeze, tyres) as soon as you can get out
