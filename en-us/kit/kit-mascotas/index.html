Who it's for. For any home with a dog or cat. In an evacuation due to fire, flood or smoke, the animal cannot stay and there is not always room for it: the difference between taking it with you and losing it is having the carrier and leash findable in 30 seconds.

When it isn't enough. It is not a kit for leaving the animal alone at home for three days: an animal cannot manage itself like a pantry. If your emergency plan involves leaving it behind, that plan does not work.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Believing your pet will eat when hungry. A frightened dog or cat will leave the dry food. Offer wet food and sit with them; don't leave them alone with a bowl.
  2. Taking them without a carrier "because they're very good". A frightened cat or dog will bite and run off; in an evacuation, a loose animal is a lost animal.
  3. Forgetting your pet's water in the count. The family calculates 3 litres per person and forgets that the dog is another consumer.
  4. Not bringing the vaccination record: without vaccines they won't be accepted at shelters or boarding facilities, and they may not be able to pass certain checks.
  5. Leaving them at home with water and food "for two days". With no ventilation, no shops open and a damaged building, two days can be the last before they drop off the list of things that are fine.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Keep the carrier and leash in the same place as your emergency backpack, not in the storage room
  • Calculate water: about 50-60 ml per kilo per day (a 15 kg dog drinks almost 1 litre a day; a cat, 200-300 ml)
  • Keep a paper copy of the health record and microchip number: they ask for it at shelters or refuges
  • Take a recent photo of the animal with you and save it on your phone and on paper: it is what proves it is yours
  • If your dog needs a muzzle because of a potentially dangerous breed, keep it next to the leash: without it they will not let you take it on certain types of transport

→ During

  • Animals sense fear: speak normally, do not pick it up if it is large, put it in the carrier without a fuss
  • Offer water every 2-3 hours; stress dehydrates more than heat
  • Do not improvise with human food: sudden diet changes cause diarrhoea just when there is no vet
  • If there is smoke or gas, wet a towel and cover its snout without covering the nose completely

→ After

  • Refill your pet's water on the same day: it's the first thing they drink and the one most often forgotten
  • Check paws and pads (glass, staples, burns) when you get home
  • Wait a few days before bathing them: their own scent gives them a sense of security in a new place
  • Write down what was missing (food they didn't like, uncomfortable bowl) and fix it before next time
