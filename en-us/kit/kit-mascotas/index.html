Who it's for. For any home with a dog or cat. In an evacuation due to fire, flood or smoke, the animal cannot stay behind and there isn't always room for it: the difference between taking it with you and losing it is having the carrier and leash findable within 30 seconds.

When it isn't enough. This is not a kit for leaving the animal alone at home for three days: an animal cannot manage itself like a pantry. If your emergency plan involves leaving and abandoning it, that plan doesn't work.

The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Thinking the animal will eat when it's hungry. A scared dog or cat leaves the kibble. Offer it wet food and sit with it; don't leave it alone with a bowl.
  2. Taking it without a carrier 'because it's very good.' A scared cat and dog bite and run off; in an evacuation, a loose animal is a lost animal.
  3. Forgetting the animal's water in the count. The family calculates 3 liters per person and forgets that the dog is another consumer.
  4. Not carrying the vaccination record: without vaccines they won't accept it in shelters or boarding facilities, and it may not be able to pass certain checks.
  5. Leaving it at home with water and food for 'two days.' With no ventilation, no shops and a damaged building, two days can be the last days before it falls off the list of things that are fine.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Keep the carrier and leash in the same place as your emergency backpack, not in the storage room
  • Work out the water: about 50-60 ml per kilo per day (a 15 kg dog drinks almost 1 litre a day; a cat, 200-300 ml)
  • Keep a paper copy of the vaccination record and the microchip number: they ask for it at a shelter or refuge
  • Take a recent photo of the animal with you and keep it on your phone and on paper: it's what proves the animal is yours
  • If your dog needs a muzzle because of a potentially dangerous breed, keep it next to the leash: without it they won't let it on certain types of transport

→ During

  • Animals sense fear: speak normally, don't pick it up if it's large, put it in the carrier without fuss
  • Offer water every 2-3 hours; stress dehydrates more than heat
  • Don't improvise with human food: sudden diet changes cause diarrhoea right when there's no vet
  • If there's smoke or gas, wet a towel and cover its snout without fully covering the nose

→ After

  • Refill the animal's water the same day: it's the first thing drunk and the most often forgotten
  • Check legs and pads (glass, staples, burns) when you get home
  • Wait a few days before bathing it: its own scent gives it a sense of security in a new place
  • Note what it lacked (food it didn't like, uncomfortable bowl) and fix it before next time
