The 5 typical mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  1. Buying a generator without calculating watts: 300 W won't start a fridge (it needs 1,200 W peak).
  2. Leaving the power bank at half charge: it self-discharges and on the day of the outage it's dead.
  3. Confusing Wh with mAh: 20,000 mAh is only 74 Wh, not 20 hours of running a fridge.
  4. Putting the solar panel in the shade or behind glass: it produces half or less.
  5. Using candles unattended: it's the leading cause of fires during power outages.

Quick guide

→ Before

  • Calculate your real wattage: add up the essentials (LED lights, router, phone, fridge if possible) and buy with some headroom.
  • Place the solar panel where it gets direct sun for most of the day; in winter it produces half as much
  • Charge EVERYTHING to 100% every Sunday: a battery at 60% on the day of the outage is a useless battery
  • Use LED lights with built-in batteries: they consume 10 times less than any alternative
  • Keep the cables together and labeled: in the dark there's no time to check which is which

→ During

  • Power lights, phone and radio first; the fridge only if your generator can handle the start-up surge
  • The router uses very little: keeping the internet alive can be worth more than a fridge
  • Recharge during the day with the panel; draw from the battery at night: never both at once
  • Turn off and unplug what you don't use: devices on standby drain the battery and give you nothing in return

→ After

  • Recharge everything to 100% again before storing the kit
  • Note down how many hours it lasted and what you used: next time you'll buy better
  • Check battery cycles: if capacity drops below 80%, it's time to replace it
