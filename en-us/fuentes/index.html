Official sources

27 public sources from official bodies, grouped by area. These are where the kit lists, timings, warnings and data for the “Your area” map come from. Every link is checked before publication (last reviewed: 2026-09-28).

European Union4

  • Estrategia de Preparación de la UE (marzo 2025)

    The document that sets 72 hours as the horizon for household self-sufficiency. This is where the whole approach of the website comes from.

  • Comisión Europea — Estrategia de la Unión de la Preparación

    Official EU portal with recommendations by risk type and citizen preparedness materials.

  • Copernicus Emergency Management Service

    EU emergency service: rapid mapping of floods, fires and disasters.

  • 112 — número europeo de emergencias

    112 works throughout the EU, free of charge and from any mobile phone. Official guide on when to call and what to say.

Civil Protection and Emergencies4

  • Dirección General de Protección Civil y Emergencias

    Main source for the site: plans, recommendations and materials from the Ministry of the Interior.

  • Protección Civil — Recomendaciones ante emergencias

    The lists of what a prepared household should have: the basis for all the kits here.

  • Protección Civil — Red de Alerta Nacional y ES-Alert

    How mobile phone alerts (ES-Alert) work when there is an emergency in your area.

  • Ministerio del Interior

    National coordination, Civil Protection and emergency response.

Health4

  • Ministerio de Sanidad

    Healthcare provision, public health recommendations and crisis preparedness.

  • Agencia Española de Seguridad Alimentaria y Nutrición (AESAN)

    Food safety: preserving food without refrigeration and safe consumption in a crisis.

  • Catálogo Nacional de Hospitales

    Official list of hospitals in Spain, with services and available beds.

  • Centro de Coordinación de Alertas Sanitarias

    Official health alerts and recommendations for the public.

Weather2

  • AEMET — Agencia Estatal de Meteorología

    Official forecast; this is where the alerts that underpin the heat, cold and DANA kits come from.

  • AEMET — Avisos meteorológicos

    Warnings by hazard and risk level: for planning before it arrives, not during.

Supplies and energy3

  • Red Eléctrica de España

    Electricity system operator: information on the grid and supply.

  • Demanda eléctrica en tiempo real (REE)

    Live demand curve. Useful for understanding a blackout or a demand spike.

  • Ministerio para la Transición Ecológica y el Reto Demográfico

    Water, energy and climate risks: the official framework for supplies.

Security3

  • Guardia Civil

    Local police posts, emergency services and response in rural areas.

  • Policía Nacional

    Police stations and emergency services in urban areas.

  • Cruz Roja Española

    Volunteer support, evacuation and psychosocial care during and after an emergency.

Regulations2

  • Boletín Oficial del Estado

    Official publication of all regulations cited on this site.

  • BOE — Directrices Básicas de Planificación de Protección Civil

    Floods, adverse weather events, wildfires and hazardous materials.

Map and site data5

  • Instituto Geográfico Nacional (IGN)

    Base map for the /zona/ page (CC BY 4.0) and official geographic data for Spain.

  • datos.gob.es — datos abiertos del Estado

    Catalog of open data sets from the Spanish public administration.

  • OpenStreetMap — licencia y colaboración

    The hospitals, pharmacies, police stations and shelters on the /zona/ map come from here (ODbL).

  • Overpass API (OpenStreetMap)

    Live OpenStreetMap query used by “Your area” to render your surroundings instantly.

  • Comisión Nacional de los Mercados y la Competencia

    Telecommunications and competition: information on coverage and communications services.

None of this replaces emergency services: in a real emergency, call 911.
